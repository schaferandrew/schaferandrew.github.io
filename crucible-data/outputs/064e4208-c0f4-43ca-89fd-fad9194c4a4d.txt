## Items the writer COULD have known about (correct for June 10, 1863)

**a) Emancipation Proclamation —** Issued/effective January 1, 1863, so it had indeed taken effect "this New Year's Day." ✗ Not anachronistic.

**b) Vicksburg —** The siege began in mid-May 1863 and would last until July 4, so in June it was genuinely dragging on. ✗ Not anachronistic.

**d) Telegraph —** The telegraph network was well established (Washington connected since 1844; transcontinental line completed 1861). ✗ Not anachronistic.

**e) Monitor vs. Merrimack —** Fought March 1862, so "last year" is exactly right from 1863. ✗ Not anachronistic.

**h) Brady/Antietam photos —** The "Dead of Antietam" photographs were displayed at Brady's New York gallery in October 1862 and caused a sensation; the writer could have heard of them. (Historical footnote: the actual exposures were by Gardner and Gibson under Brady's auspices.) ✗ Not anachronistic.

**i) Stonewall Jackson —** Died May 10, 1863, so "last month" is correct. ✗ Not anachronistic.

## Items the writer could NOT have known or done

**c) Gettysburg —** The battle was fought July 1–3, 1863 — *after* the letter's date. The farmer could not report a defeat that hadn't happened.

**f) Telephone —** Not invented until 1876 (Bell's patent). No one was telephoning anyone in 1863.

**g) Barbed wire —** Not invented/patented until 1874 (Glidden, Haish, etc.). No barbed wire existed for fencing in 1863.

**j) West Virginia —** Admitted to the Union June 20, 1863 — *ten days after* the letter's date, not "last month."

**k) Draft riots —** The New York draft riots occurred July 13–16, 1863, a month after the letter was written.

ANACHRONISMS: c, f, g, j, k
