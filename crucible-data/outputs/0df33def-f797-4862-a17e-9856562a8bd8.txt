The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, with Council member Ines Varga voting no because the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation" and noted that collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24 [S1, S2]. Columnist Rob Haddad argues in an opinion column that the transit levy is a "tax grab" that will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, stating, "Nobody asked the people paying for it" [S9].

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, injuring two firefighters according to an initial report, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected that one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, with crews having the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. while the cause remains under investigation [S3, S4].

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with Ledger reporters estimating the crowd at about 300, organizers claiming more than 1,000 people attended, and police estimating the crowd at 400 [S5, S6].

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, with a spokesperson saying Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week so the increase would take effect in the fall term [S7].

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1% [S8].
