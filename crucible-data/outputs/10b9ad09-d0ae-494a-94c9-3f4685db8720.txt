The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, with Mayor Theo Brandt calling it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation" and collection beginning July 1 if the measure passes its second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga voted no, stating the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while columnist Rob Haddad argues that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore where Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released; earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening with conflicting attendance estimates as Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300 while organizers said more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, with Governor Lena Ruiz expected to sign it this week so the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]
