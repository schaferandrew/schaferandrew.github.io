The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy over ten years on a 7-2 vote late Monday, allocating funds for three new bus routes and rebuilding the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," with collections set to begin July 1 if the measure passes its second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga opposed the levy, citing the lack of published household cost estimates. A separate opinion column by Rob Haddad in The Ardmore Bugle argued the levy amounts to a "tax grab," claiming it would cost households $900 annually and criticized the council for rushing the vote without public input [S1, S2, S9 opinion].  

A fire erupted early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, initially reported as injuring two firefighters. Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected this, stating one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, while crews contained the blaze by 9:15 a.m. The cause remains under investigation [S3, S4].  

A rally supporting a proposed rent stabilization ordinance drew an estimated 300 attendees outside City Hall on Monday evening, according to Ardmore Ledger reporters, though organizers claimed over 1,000 participants and police estimated 400 [S5, S6].  

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, with the increase taking effect in the fall term [S7].  

Veloria’s central bank raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, citing 4.1% inflation as the reason for its third increase this year [S8].
