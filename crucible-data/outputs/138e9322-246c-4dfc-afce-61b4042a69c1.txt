The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and Lakeshore Public Radio reported that collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, and the levy still needs a second reading on March 24. In an opinion column, The Ardmore Bugle's Rob Haddad argues that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said, with the increase taking effect in the fall term. [S7]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. An initial report from the scene said two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah corrected that one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released; crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300; organizers said more than 1,000 people attended, while police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
