To solve the problem of finding the probability that both children are boys given that at least one of them is a boy born on a Tuesday, we need to carefully consider the sample space and the conditions provided.

First, let's define the sample space for each child. Each child can be either a boy (B) or a girl (G), and they can be born on any day of the week (Mon-Sun). Therefore, there are \(2 \times 7 = 14\) possible outcomes for each child. For two children, the total number of outcomes is \(14^2 = 196\).

Next, we need to determine the number of favorable outcomes where at least one child is a boy born on a Tuesday (BT). Let's denote this event as \(A\). The probability of a child being BT is \(\frac{1}{14}\).

The number of outcomes where at least one child is BT can be calculated using the principle of inclusion-exclusion:
\[
|A| = |B_1T| + |B_2T| - |B_1TB_2T|
\]
where \(B_1T\) is the event that the first child is BT, \(B_2T\) is the event that the second child is BT, and \(B_1TB_2T\) is the event that both children are BT.

Calculating each term:
\[
|B_1T| = 1 \times 14 = 14 \quad \text{(first child is BT, second child can be any outcome)}
\]
\[
|B_2T| = 14 \times 1 = 14 \quad \text{(second child is BT, first child can be any outcome)}
\]
\[
|B_1TB_2T| = 1 \times 1 = 1 \quad \text{(both children are BT)}
\]

Thus,
\[
|A| = 14 + 14 - 1 = 27
\]

Now, we need to find the number of outcomes where both children are boys (BB) and at least one is BT. Let's denote this event as \(B\). The probability of a child being a boy is \(\frac{1}{2}\), and the probability of a boy being born on Tuesday is \(\frac{1}{7}\).

The number of outcomes where both children are boys is:
\[
|BB| = 7 \times 7 = 49 \quad \text{(each child can be any day, but must be a boy)}
\]

Among these 49 outcomes, the number of outcomes where at least one child is BT is:
\[
|A \cap B| = |B_1T| + |B_2T| - |B_1TB_2T|
\]
where \(B_1T\) is the event that the first child is BT and the second child is a boy, \(B_2T\) is the event that the second child is BT and the first child is a boy, and \(B_1TB_2T\) is the event that both children are BT.

Calculating each term:
\[
|B_1T| = 1 \times 7 = 7 \quad \text{(first child is BT, second child can be any day but must be a boy)}
\]
\[
|B_2T| = 7 \times 1 = 7 \quad \text{(second child is BT, first child can be any day but must be a boy)}
\]
\[
|B_1TB_2T| = 1 \times 1 = 1 \quad \text{(both children are BT)}
\]

Thus,
\[
|A \cap B| = 7 + 7 - 1 = 13
\]

Finally, the probability that both children are boys given that at least one is a boy born on a Tuesday is:
\[
P(B | A) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A|} = \frac{13}{27}
\]

Therefore, the answer is \(\boxed{\frac{13}{27}}\).
