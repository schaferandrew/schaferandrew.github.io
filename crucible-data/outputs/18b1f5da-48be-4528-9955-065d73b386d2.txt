Port Ardmore’s City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years on a 7-2 vote late Monday. The money will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station; the levy still needs a second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. Mayor Theo Brandt called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading. In an opinion column, Rob Haddad argued the levy would cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing, “Nobody asked the people paying for it.” [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street. Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, correcting an earlier report that two firefighters were injured. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m.; the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said; the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Ardmore Ledger reporters estimated the crowd at about 300; organizers told Lakeshore Public Radio more than 1,000 people attended, while police estimated 400. [S5, S6]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
