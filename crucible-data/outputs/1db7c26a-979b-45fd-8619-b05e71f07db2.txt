The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, funding three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station; council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, and the levy still needs a second reading on March 24. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading. [S1, S2] In an opinion column, Ardmore Bugle columnist Rob Haddad argues the levy is a tax grab, claiming it will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S9 opinion] A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore; Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, correcting earlier reports that two firefighters were injured, and crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. while the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4] Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening; Ardmore Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300, while organizers said more than 1,000 people attended, and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6] The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, and Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said, with the increase taking effect in the fall term. [S7] The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
