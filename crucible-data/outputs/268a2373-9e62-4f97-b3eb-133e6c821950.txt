## Analysis of Each Item (Letter dated June 10, 1863, Ohio)

**a) Emancipation Proclamation "this New Year's Day"** – No problem. The Proclamation took effect on January 1, 1863, so "this New Year's Day" is a perfectly natural reference from June.

**b) Siege of Vicksburg "dragging on"** – No problem. The siege began May 18, 1863; by June 10 it had been going three weeks and news was reaching the North.

**c) Lee's defeat at Gettysburg** – **Impossible.** Gettysburg was fought July 1–3, 1863, more than three weeks in the future. No one could have reported the result.

**d) Telegraph bringing news from Washington** – No problem. The telegraph network was fully operational and widely used by 1863.

**e) Monitor's fight with the Merrimack "last year"** – No problem. The Battle of Hampton Roads was March 9, 1862, which is indeed "last year" from mid-1863.

**f) A cousin who "telephoned"** – **Impossible.** Alexander Graham Bell did not patent the telephone until 1876. No telephone existed in 1863.

**g) Fencing with barbed wire** – **Impossible.** Joseph Glidden patented barbed wire in 1874. In 1863 a farmer would have used wooden rails or woven wire, not barbed wire.

**h) Mathew Brady's photographs of the dead at Antietam** – No problem. Antietam was September 17, 1862; Brady's images were circulated and exhibited well before June 1863.

**i) Stonewall Jackson's death "last month"** – No problem. Jackson died May 10, 1863, which is indeed "last month" from June 10.

**j) West Virginia "joined the Union last month"** – **Impossible.** West Virginia was admitted on **June 20, 1863**, ten days *after* this letter. It had not yet joined, and certainly not "last month" (May).

**k) The draft riots in New York** – **Impossible.** The New York draft riots erupted July 13–16, 1863, over a month after the letter's date.
