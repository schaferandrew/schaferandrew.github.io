**Reasoning**

A 34-year-old was born around 1990–91, so her parents were naming her in the late '80s/early '90s. By that generation, most working-class Polish-American families in the upper Midwest (Milwaukee, Gary, Duluth, the Driftless Area, small towns in Iowa and the Dakotas) had fully Americanized first names but kept the surname. Some still slipped a Polish first name in, especially if it was a grandmother's or great-grandmother's name. The surname is the real marker of heritage here, and it should feel *inherited*—a name you've had since you were a baby, not a costume.

I wanted to avoid the "Kasia Kowalski" / "Wanda Nowak" trap, where the name does the work of signaling ethnicity in a reductive, almost comic way. Instead, I leaned on: (a) surnames that are genuinely common in those communities but not the single most obvious one, (b) first names that are period-appropriate and unglamorous, and (c) a few Polish first names that a family might actually keep in rotation. The character's interiority—dry, guarded, sharp—should be *suggested* by the phonetics and connotations of the name, not stated.
