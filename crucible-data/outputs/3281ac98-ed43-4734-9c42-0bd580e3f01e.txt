**e)** Could have known. The clash between the *Monitor* and *Merrimack* (CSS *Virginia*) occurred in March 1862, so it was indeed “last year.”

**f)** Could **NOT** have done. A practical telephone was not invented until Alexander Graham Bell’s patent in 1876.

**g)** Could **NOT** have done. Barbed wire was not patented or commercially available until after the Civil War (late 1860s and 1870s).

**h)** Could have known. Photographs of the Antietam dead (taken by Gardner for Brady’s studio) were exhibited in New York in October 1862 and widely reported.

**i)** Could have known. Stonewall Jackson died on May 10, 1863; news of his death spread quickly via telegraph a month before this letter.

**j)** Could **NOT** have known. West Virginia was not admitted to the Union until June 20, 1863—ten days after the date of the letter.

**k)** Could **NOT** have known. The New York City draft riots did not erupt until July 13–16, 1863.
