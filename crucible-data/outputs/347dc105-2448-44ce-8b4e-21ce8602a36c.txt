## Reasoning

Two clear errors stand out:

1. **"then"** → should be **"than."** This is a comparison ("louder *than* she remembered"), not a reference to time, so "then" is wrong regardless of voice or rhythm.
2. **"to fast"** → should be **"too fast."** The intended meaning is excess speed; "to" here is simply a misspelling of "too."

The rest reads cleanly and the fragments ("Not waiting for her, exactly.") and em dash in "Still—she was back." are stylistic choices I'm preserving.

One judgment call: the comma before **"or"** in "whether to laugh, or leave." In a simple two-item list, standard usage omits it, but some writers insert it deliberately to force a pause right at the moment of decision—which fits this scene's tension. So rather than silently removing it, I'm flagging it for your call.

CORRECTED:
Mara stepped off the train and into a city that smelled like rain, hot asphalt, and somebody's breakfast. The station was louder than she remembered it, all brakes shrieking and people talking too fast. She could feel the old neighborhood somewhere beyond the buildings, waiting. Not waiting for her, exactly. That would have been too kind. Still—she was back. And she didn't know whether to laugh or leave.

POSSIBLY INTENTIONAL:
"laugh, or leave": The comma before "or" isn't standard in a two-item list, but it could be deliberate—a forced pause at the exact moment of indecision, which suits the scene's tension. Left as written unless you want it removed.
