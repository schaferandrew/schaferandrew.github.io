

Reasoning: I prioritized first names that were statistically common for girls born around 1990 in the upper Midwest, avoiding overly trendy or archaic choices that would break the contemporary literary fiction frame. Surnames were drawn from assimilated or regionally authentic Polish-American families, deliberately steering clear of caricatured, overly exoticized, or stereotype-laden spellings. Each name’s phonetic quality and cultural resonance was matched to the character’s core traits: crisp or understated sounds for emotional guardedness, classical or precise connotations for high intelligence, and grounded, working-class cadences for ambition and dry humor. The selections lean toward literary fiction conventions—names that feel lived-in, psychologically open, and culturally specific without being reductive.
