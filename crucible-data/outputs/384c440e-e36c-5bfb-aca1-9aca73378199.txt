Here are six geography questions, each with a single answer and a one-sentence justification for why the obvious answer is incorrect:

1. Which country is crossed by both the Equator and the Tropic of Capricorn? (Obvious answer might be Ecuador or Brazil, but they are often thought to only cross one.)
2. Which country has coastline on both the Red Sea and the Persian Gulf? (Obvious answer might be Egypt, but it lacks a direct connection to both bodies of water.)
3. What is the northernmost capital city of a sovereign state? (Obvious answer might be Reykjavik, but its status or location relative to other capitals can be debated.)
4. Counting overseas territories, which country spans the most time zones? (Obvious answer might be Russia or China, but their contiguous landmasses are less extensive than the chosen nation's claims.)
5. Which US state is closest to mainland Africa? (Obvious answer might be Florida, but its proximity is often overstated compared to other locations.)
6. Apart from the microstates inside Italy, which is the only landlocked country that borders exactly one other country? (Obvious answer might be Switzerland, but its neighbors are numerous.)

***

**ANSWERS**
1. Brazil (While parts of South America fit the description, Brazil is the vast country whose territory encompasses both lines.)
2. Yemen (This nation uniquely possesses coastlines on both the Arabian Sea/Indian Ocean access point and the Gulf.)
3. Nuuk (The capital of Greenland, which is a sovereign territory under the Kingdom of Denmark, holds this title.)
4. France (Through its numerous overseas territories scattered across the globe, France maintains claims spanning many time zones.)
5. Hawaii (While Florida is geographically closer to the tropics, Hawaii's chain extends into the warmest waters bordering Africa.)
6. Uzbekistan (This Central Asian nation shares a border with only one country, though its neighbors are often more complex.)
