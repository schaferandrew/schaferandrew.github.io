## Port Ardmore briefing — Tuesday, March 10

### Transit levy advances
The Port Ardmore City Council approved a **$41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years** on a **7-2** vote late Monday, according to the **Ardmore Ledger** and **Lakeshore Public Radio**. The money would fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. The levy needs a second reading on **March 24**; collection would begin **July 1 if the levy passes its second reading**, Lakeshore Public Radio reported.

Council member **Ines Varga**, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, the Ledger reported. Mayor **Theo Brandt** called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” Lakeshore Public Radio reported.

### Kettle Street warehouse fire
**Statewide Wire** initially reported that two firefighters were injured in an early Tuesday fire at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street. In an update, **Lakeshore Public Radio** reported Fire Chief **Ama Mensah** said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released; the earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect. Crews had the fire under control by **9:15 a.m.** The cause remains under investigation.

### Teacher pay bill passes legislature
The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to **$48,500**, **Statewide Wire** reported. The vote was **39-28 in the Senate** and **81-52 in the House**. Governor **Lena Ruiz** is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said; the increase would take effect in the fall term.

### Rent stabilization rally crowd estimates differ
Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. The **Ardmore Ledger** estimated the crowd at **about 300**. **Lakeshore Public Radio** reported that organizers said **more than 1,000** attended, while police estimated **400**.

### International
The **Global Dispatch** reported that Veloria’s central bank raised its benchmark interest rate by **0.25 percentage points to 3.75%**, its third increase this year, citing inflation of **4.1%**.

### Opinion — separate from reporting
In an opinion column, **The Ardmore Bugle**’s **Rob Haddad** called the transit levy “a tax grab.” He argued it would cost a typical household **$900 a year**, that the council rushed the vote, and wrote, “Nobody asked the people paying for it.”
