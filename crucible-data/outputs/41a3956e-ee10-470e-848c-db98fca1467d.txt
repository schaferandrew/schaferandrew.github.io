The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy with a 7-2 vote, funding three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station; Council member Ines Varga opposed it, citing lack of household cost estimates, and the levy must pass a second reading on March 24 [S1, S2]. Mayor Theo Brandt praised the decision as the city’s largest bus investment in generations and said collections would begin in July if the measure passes its second reading [S2]. A fire broke out at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street early Tuesday, with initial reports indicating two firefighters were injured; however, fire chief Ama Mensah later clarified that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, with crews controlling the blaze by 9:15 a.m. [S3, S4]. Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with estimates of attendance ranging from 300 by Ledger reporters to over 1,000 by organizers and 400 by police [S5, S6]. The state legislature passed a bill increasing the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with Governor Lena Ruiz expected to sign it soon, effective in the fall term [S7]. The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third increase this year amid a reported inflation rate of 4.1% [S8]. Columnist Rob Haddad in The Ardmore Bugle criticized the transit levy as a tax grab, arguing that it will cost a typical household $900 annually and that the council rushed the vote without public input [S9 opinion]. Delphine Okoro won an open seat on the Port Ardmore school board with 52% of the vote, according to results from March 3 [S10].
