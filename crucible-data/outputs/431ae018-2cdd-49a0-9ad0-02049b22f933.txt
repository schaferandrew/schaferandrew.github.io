

The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, with the funds designated for three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt described the measure as the biggest investment in buses the city has made in a generation, noting that collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga voted against the measure, stating that the city had not published a cost estimate for households. Columnist Rob Haddad of The Ardmore Bugle argued that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing that nobody asked the people paying for it. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, prompting an emergency response that initially reported two firefighters were injured. Fire Chief Ama Mensah corrected the earlier account, stating only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance gathered outside City Hall on Monday evening to rally for the measure. Crowd estimates disagreed, with the Ardmore Ledger estimating about 300 attendees, police estimating 400 people, and rally organizers claiming more than 1,000 individuals participated. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, securing votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. A spokesperson said Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign the legislation this week, with the increase taking effect during the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third increase this year as it cited inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
