The main traps are counting the multiples of 21 correctly in #1 and the classic 1/3 or 1/2 mistake in #5.

**1. Divisible by 3 or 7, but not by 21**

- Multiples of 3: ⌊10000/3⌋ = 3333
- Multiples of 7: ⌊10000/7⌋ = 1428
- Multiples of 21: ⌊10000/21⌋ = 476

By inclusion–exclusion, the count divisible by 3 or 7 is 3333 + 1428 − 476 = 4285.

The multiples of 21 are all inside that set, so remove them: 4285 − 476 = **3809**.

The tempting error is to subtract the multiples of 21 only once, giving 4285. They must be subtracted once for inclusion–exclusion and again to exclude them.

**2. 7^2026 mod 100**

7^2 = 49, and 7^4 = 2401 ≡ 1 (mod 100).

Since 2026 = 4·506 + 2, we get 7^2026 ≡ 7^2 = **49** (mod 100).

**3. Tilings of a 2×12 board**

Look at the left end of the board. It is covered in one of three ways:
- one vertical domino, leaving a 2×(n−1) board;
- two stacked horizontal dominoes, leaving a 2×(n−2) board;
- one 2×2 square, leaving a 2×(n−2) board.

So aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + 2aₙ₋₂, with a₀ = 1 and a₁ = 1.

The sequence is 1, 1, 3, 5, 11, 21, 43, 85, 171, 341, 683, 1365, 2731.

As a check, the closed form (2^(n+1) + (−1)^n)/3 gives 8193/3 = 2731. So a₁₂ = **2731**.

**4. Largest of three dice is exactly 4**

P(max ≤ 4) − P(max ≤ 3) = (4³ − 3³)/6³ = (64 − 27)/216 = **37/216**.

**5. At least one boy born on Tuesday**

Each child has 14 equally likely sex/day types, so there are 196 ordered pairs.

- Pairs with no Tuesday-boy: 13² = 169.
- Pairs with at least one Tuesday-boy: 196 − 169 = 27.

Among those 27, count the boy–boy pairs:
- There are 7² = 49 boy–boy pairs in total.
- Of these, 6² = 36 have no Tuesday-boy.
- So 49 − 36 = 13 boy–boy pairs qualify.

The probability is **13/27**. This is neither 1/3 nor 1/2. The day-of-week detail matters because it makes the condition more specific.

**ANSWERS**
1. 3809
2. 49
3. 2731
4. 37/216
5. 13/27
