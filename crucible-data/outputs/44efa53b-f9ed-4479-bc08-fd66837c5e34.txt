### 1. Token game

Let \(F(n,p)\) mean that the player to move can win with \(n\) tokens remaining, where the preceding move removed \(p\) tokens. Use \(p=0\) for the unrestricted first move. Then
\[
F(n,p)=\bigvee_{\substack{m\in\{2,3,5,7\}\\m\le n,\ m\ne p}}\neg F(n-m,m).
\]
An empty set of legal moves makes \(F(n,p)\) false.

Evaluating this recurrence, the losing initial positions through 100 are
\[
1,9,13,17,25,26,34,42,43,51,59,60,68,76,77,85,93,94.
\]
There are **18**.

### 2. Pins

A standing run of \(n\) pins has Grundy value
\[
g(n)=\operatorname{mex}\left\{
g(a)\mathbin{\oplus}g(n-k-a):
k\in\{2,3\},\ 0\le a\le n-k
\right\},
\]
with \(g(0)=g(1)=0\). Separate runs combine by XOR.

For lengths 0 through 21, the values are
\[
0,0,1,1,2,2,3,3,1,1,4,4,3,3,2,2,1,1,4,4,2,2.
\]

A winning first move leaves two runs of equal Grundy value. The winning moves are:

- Remove two pins, starting at position **11 or 12**.
- Remove three pins, starting at position **4, 6, 8, 11, 14, 16 or 18**.

Thus there are **9** winning first moves.

### 3. Graph game

For undirected vertex geography, a starting vertex is winning exactly when every maximum matching covers it. This follows from the strategy of responding along matching edges.

Here, \(\{5,6,7,8\}\) is an independent set whose neighbors are only \(\{1,10,11\}\). Consequently, every matching leaves at least one of those four vertices uncovered.

A matching of size five exists:
\[
\{8\!-\!10,\ 5\!-\!11,\ 1\!-\!7,\ 2\!-\!3,\ 4\!-\!9\}.
\]
Moreover, each of \(5,6,7,8\) can individually be the uncovered vertex in a size-five matching. Thus the winning starting vertices are exactly
\[
1,2,3,4,9,10,11.
\]
Their sum is **40**.

### 4. Knights and knaves

Write each person's type as a Boolean, with 1 meaning knight. The statements give
\[
B=\neg F,\qquad D=(A=F),\qquad
E=[A+D+F=2].
\]
For every choice of \(A,F\), the sum \(A+D+F\) is either 1 or 3. Hence \(E=0\).

It follows that \(C=\neg G\), while F's statement requires \(F\Rightarrow G\). G's statement permits \(G=0\) only when \(C=D=1\). Finally, \(H=A\).

The complete list of consistent assignments is below; anyone not listed is a knave.

| Knights | Number |
|---|---:|
| B, D, G | 3 |
| B, C, D | 3 |
| F, G | 2 |
| A, B, G, H | 4 |
| A, D, F, G, H | 5 |

The total is \(3+3+2+4+5=\mathbf{17}\).

### 6. Talk schedules

Since S is before P and P lies between R and S, the order must be
\[
S<P<R.
\]
The nonconsecutive condition gives \(p-s\ge2\). Together with \(p\ne5\), this leaves only \(p=3,4,6\).

For each placement of S, P, R and T, count the available slots for Q, excluding slots 1 and 3. The remaining two slots can hold U and V in two orders.

| P's slot | Placements including Q, before ordering U and V | Complete schedules |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 15 | 30 |
| 4 | 22 | 44 |

Thus the total is \(30+44+48=\mathbf{122}\).
