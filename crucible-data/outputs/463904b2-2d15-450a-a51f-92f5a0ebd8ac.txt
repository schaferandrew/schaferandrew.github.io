## Reasoning

**Language errors (objective grammar/spelling/agreement/word choice):**

- **3.** Subject–verb agreement: "The prices … *is* weighted" — "prices" is plural, so the verb must be "are."
- **6.** Possessive error: "*it's*" (*it is*) should be "**its**," the possessive form.
- **12.** Wrong comparative word: "more expensive *then*" should be "than" (comparative conjunction), not "then" (adverb of time).

Sentences 2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 16, and 17 are grammatically sound and their claims are defensible as stated (the Fed's target is indeed 2%; the natural rate estimate is hedged with "estimate" and "around"; QE's definition is standard; QE began in late 2008; Japan did stagnate after its bubble burst; supply-side theory is described accurately; Reagan cut the top rate from ~70% to 28%; deficits did rise in the 1980s). No error.

**Overstated / imprecise / misleading claims:**

- **1.** *imprecise* — Defines inflation as merely "prices go up" and attributes it solely to government money printing, ignoring that inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level over time and can stem from demand-pull or supply shocks without monetary expansion.
- **4.** *misleading* — There is no legal rule tying CPI above 2% to rate hikes; the 2% target is a policy goal, not an automatic statutory trigger.
- **8.** *overstated* — A false dichotomy ("lazy or victim of bad policy") overgeneralizes and ignores other causes (wage rigidity, demand shortfalls, etc.).
- **11.** *overstated* — QE does not "inevitably" cause hyperinflation; historically it has not.
- **14.** *overstated* — Absolute terms ("completely ineffective," "proves") overstate what Japan's experience shows about monetary policy.
- **18.** *misleading* — The economy did grow in the 1980s, so "without growing the economy at all" is factually wrong; "definitively debunked" also overstates the verdict.
