## The Top Five Fits & Why They Work

If I had to narrow it down based on the description (Intelligent, Dryly Funny, Guarded, Working-Class Roots, Polish-American background), these five combinations offer the best balance:

**1. Clara Hayes:**
* **Why it fits:** "Clara" is classic, strong, and intellectual without being fussy. It suggests clarity of thought (fitting her intelligence) but is also simple enough to suggest the grounded, no-nonsense upbringing of the Upper Midwest. It feels enduring and understated—perfect for someone who observes before she speaks.

**2. Margot Richter:**
* **Why it fits:** "Margot" has a slightly European, sophisticated edge that hints at the Polish heritage without screaming it. It feels chosen rather than accidental. Paired with a strong, slightly severe surname like "Richter," it conveys her ambition and emotional armor immediately.

**3. Vivian Sokolowski:**
* **Why it fits:** "Vivian" carries a quiet vibrancy, suggesting the intelligence bubbling beneath the surface. If you use a surname like Sokolowski, it grounds her heritage while the first name keeps her sounding modern and literary. It suggests someone who has seen things but chooses to remain composed.

**4. Eleanor Hayes:**
* **Why it fits:** "Eleanor" is weighty, serious, and inherently intelligent. It suggests a deep thoughtfulness that matches the literary fiction tone. It’s a name that doesn't offer easy warmth, fitting her guarded nature perfectly; she has depth, but it takes effort to reach it.

**5. Tessa Miller:**
* **Why it fits:** This is the most grounded option. "Tessa" feels accessible, fitting the working-class roots. However, when paired with a sharp intelligence and her dry wit, it becomes ironic—she is deceptively plain on the surface but complex underneath. It suggests she has had to *earn* her intellectual space.
