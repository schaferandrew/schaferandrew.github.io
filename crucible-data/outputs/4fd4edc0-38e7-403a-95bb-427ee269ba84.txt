The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. The levy still needs a second reading on March 24, after which collection would begin July 1 if it passes. Council member Ines Varga voted no, saying the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." Ardmore Bugle columnist Rob Haddad argues in an opinion column that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing "Nobody asked the people paying for it" [S1, S2, S9 opinion].

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. An initial report from the scene said two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected that account, saying one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released and that earlier reports of two injuries were incorrect. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation [S3, S4].

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300, while organizers said more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400 [S5, S6].

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, according to a spokesperson, and the increase would take effect in the fall term [S7].
