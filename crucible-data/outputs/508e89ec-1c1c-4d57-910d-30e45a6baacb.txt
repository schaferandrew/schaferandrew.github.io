The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy on Monday, split over ten years to fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station; Ines Varga voted no, citing lack of cost estimates for households [S1].  
Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," with collection beginning July 1 if passed [S2].  
The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with Senate and House votes of 39-28 and 81-52 respectively; Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week [S7].  
Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with the Ardmore Ledger estimating about 300 attendees [S5].  
Organizers claimed more than 1,000 people attended, while police estimated the crowd at 400 [S6].  
A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street; two firefighters were injured according to an initial report [S3], but Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one was treated for smoke inhalation and released, with crews having it under control by 9:15 a.m. [S4].  
The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, citing inflation of 4.1% [S8].  
Colonel Rob Haddad argues the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing "Nobody asked the people paying for it" [S9 opinion].
