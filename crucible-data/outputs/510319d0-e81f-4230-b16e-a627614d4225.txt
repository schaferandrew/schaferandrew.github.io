The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years to fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. The levy still needs a second reading on March 24; collection would begin July 1 if it passes. Mayor Theo Brandt called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation.” Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. In an opinion column, Rob Haddad argues that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote; that household figure is his claim, not a reported city estimate. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. In a later update correcting the initial injury report, Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said. The increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, but attendance estimates differed. Ardmore Ledger reporters at the scene estimated about 300 people; Lakeshore Public Radio reported that organizers said more than 1,000 attended, while police estimated 400. [S5, S6]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
