Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy on Monday, a vote of seven to two that will fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” though council member Ines Varga said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. The levy still needs a second reading on March 24. Rob Haddad argues that the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing “Nobody asked the people paying for it.” [S1, S2, S9 opinion]  

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. Two firefighters were injured according to an initial report from the scene. Fire Chief Ama Mensah later said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released; earlier reports that two were injured were incorrect, and crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. [S3, S4]  

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. The Ardmore Ledger reported an estimated crowd of about 300. Organizers said more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]  

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with Senate votes of 39-28 and House votes of 81-52. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said. [S7]  

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
