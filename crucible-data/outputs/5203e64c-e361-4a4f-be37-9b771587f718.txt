1. To find how many integers \( x \) with \( 1 \leq x \leq N \) satisfy \( x^{12} \equiv 1 \pmod{N} \), we need to consider the order of \( x \) modulo \( N \). The order is the smallest positive integer \( d \) such that \( x^d \equiv 1 \pmod{N} \). For a prime \( p \), the order of any element \( x \) modulo \( p \) must divide \( p-1 \). Since \( N = 6,604,416 \), we need to find the smallest \( d \) such that \( x^d \equiv 1 \pmod{N} \). This is equivalent to finding the order of \( x \) modulo \( N \).

4. To find the number of subsets of \(\{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 20\}\) that contain no two elements differing by exactly 1 or by exactly 4, we can use a combinatorial approach. We need to count all possible pairs of numbers that differ by 1 or 4 and then subtract the number of such pairs from \( 20 \times 20 - 9 \) (since there are 9 pairs that differ by 1).
