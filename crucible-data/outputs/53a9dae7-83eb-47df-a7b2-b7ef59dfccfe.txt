

The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station and still requires a second reading on March 24 before collection can begin July 1 if it passes. Mayor Theo Brandt called the measure "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," while Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. In an opinion column, columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing, "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, and Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week according to a spokesperson, with the increase set to take effect in the fall term. [S7]

A later report corrected an earlier one regarding injuries at a fire that broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street: while an initial report said two firefighters were injured, Fire Chief Ama Mensah later said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the blaze under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Sources disagree on the size of a crowd that rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening in support of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance: Ledger reporters at the scene estimated it at about 300, organizers said more than 1,000 people attended, and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third increase this year and citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
