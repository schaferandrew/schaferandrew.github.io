**Reasoning approach.** A working-class Polish-American woman in the upper Midwest is most often *not* a person wearing her heritage as costume—many families anglicized their names, and those who kept theirs did so quietly rather than theatrically. So I've split the list between authentically Polish forms (which are simply common American names to this generation) and assimilated/bridging names, avoiding the exoticized or dated choices that read as caricature in contemporary fiction. The best names should feel like a real person's name, not a signal.

NAMES:
1. Anna Kowalski — Anna is Poland's most common female name; Kowalski is its single most frequent surname (the Polish equivalent of "Smith"). Grounded and unpretentious, fitting a family that never hid its roots.
2. Katarzyna Nowak — The full Polish spelling signals she kept her heritage intact rather than anglicizing it; Nowak is the second most common Polish surname. Deliberate, intelligent naming.
3. Helena Wójcik — Carries literary weight (Troy, Keller) and was popular in Poland; a common working-class surname keeps it from feeling ornate.
4. Barbara Kamiński — A classic American name with deep Polish roots; the feminine Kamiński shows an assimilated family that kept one thread.
5. Zofia Lewandowski — Zofia (Sophia) is elegant and distinctly Polish; Lewandowski leans educated, hinting at ambition.
6. Wanda Mazur — Quintessentially Polish but can feel dated to younger readers; the surname references a folk-dance region.
7. Kazimiera Dąbrowski — Old-fashioned and unmistakably Polish; the historical family name adds gravity.
8. Stanisława Szymański — Strong and unapologetically Polish, though it risks reading as caricature in modern fiction.
9. Aleksandra Wiśniewski — Bridges American and Polish naming at once; the feminine surname suggests assimilation with a retained thread.
10. Małgorzata Kozłowski — Beautiful and uncommon enough to stand out in literary fiction while remaining authentically Polish.
11. Ewa Zieliński — Simple and universal (Eve/Eva); the feminine surname shows assimilation with heritage intact.
12. Danuta Kamińska — Distinctive and less common, so it feels fresh rather than stereotypical.
13. Krystyna Kowalczyk — Bridges American and Polish naming; a common working-class surname keeps it real.
14. Sylwia Nowak — Elegant with a slightly literary quality while staying authentically Polish.
15. Izabela Wójcik — Both American (Isabel/Elizabeth) and Polish; the surname grounds it in working-class reality.
16. Natalia Lewandowska — Shared across Slavic cultures, giving a subtle rather than obvious ethnic signal.
17. Emilia Dąbrowska — Common in America and Poland; the feminine surname adds literary resonance.
18. Agnieszka Szymańska — Distinctive Polish (Agnes) that avoids caricature while remaining authentic.
19. Maria Kamińska — Universal and unpretentious; the feminine surname shows assimilation with retained heritage.
