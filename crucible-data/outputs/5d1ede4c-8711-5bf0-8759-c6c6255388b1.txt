### The Top 5 Explanations

**1. Elena**
This is a "bridge" name. It is common enough in Polish-American communities to feel authentic to her heritage, but it carries a sophisticated, international weight that suits her intelligence and ambition. It feels like a name a working-class mother would choose for a daughter she hoped would "go further."

**2. Margot**
Margot perfectly captures the "dryly funny" and "emotionally guarded" traits. It is a sharp, slightly vintage name that suggests a woman who is self-aware and perhaps a bit cynical. It feels like the name of someone who works very hard to maintain a polished exterior.

**3. Renee**
This is a quintessential "Upper Midwest" name. It feels grounded and honest, reflecting her working-class roots. For a literary fiction character, it provides a great contrast: she might have a "plain" name, but her internal life and intellectual output are anything but plain.

**4. Claire**
Claire is a "sharp" name. It sounds efficient and clear, mirroring a personality that is guarded and ambitious. It doesn't lean into any specific ethnic tropes, allowing her Polish-American heritage to be a part of her history rather than her primary identifier.

**5. Vera**
Vera is a subtle nod to her Polish roots without being a caricature. It is a sturdy, "no-nonsense" name. It fits a woman who is emotionally guarded—it’s a name that doesn't give much away, much like the character herself.
