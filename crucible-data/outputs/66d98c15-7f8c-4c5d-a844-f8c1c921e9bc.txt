1. Factor
\[
6{,}604{,}416=2^7\cdot3^4\cdot7^2\cdot13.
\]
By the Chinese remainder theorem, the number of solutions is the product of the local root counts. For odd prime powers, that count is \(\gcd(12,\varphi(p^k))\), giving \(6,6,12\) for \(3^4,7^2,13\). The units modulo \(2^7\) form \(C_2\times C_{32}\), giving \(2\cdot\gcd(12,32)=8\) roots. Thus the total is \(8\cdot6\cdot6\cdot12=3456\).

2. Label the copies temporarily and use inclusion–exclusion on events that a same-letter pair is adjacent. For a letter with \(r\) copies, the valid selected-edge contributions form the polynomials
\[
P_4(t)=1-12t+36t^2-24t^3,\quad
P_3(t)=1-6t+6t^2,\quad
P_2(t)=1-2t.
\]
Their product is \(1-20t+150t^2-540t^3+984t^4-864t^5+288t^6\). Weighting the coefficient of \(t^j\) by \((11-j)!\) gives \(4{,}371{,}840\) labeled arrangements. Divide by \(4!3!2!2!=576\), yielding \(7590\).

3. Let \(E_i\) be the expected number of additional rolls when the current roll is \(i\). Continuing-roll transitions give
\[
\begin{aligned}
5E_1-E_3-E_5&=6,&5E_2-E_4-E_6&=6,\\
5E_3-E_1-E_5-E_6&=6,&5E_4-E_2-E_5-E_6&=6,\\
5E_5-E_1-E_3-E_4&=6,&5E_6-E_2-E_3-E_4&=6.
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding the reduced equations gives \(E_3+E_4+E_5+E_6=144/13\), and the first two equations then give \(E_1+E_2=(12+144/13)/5\). Including the first roll, the expected total is \(47/13\), so \(m+n=60\).

4. Let \(g_n\) count valid subsets of \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) whose largest element is \(n\). Then
\[
g_n=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}g_k-\mathbf1_{n\ge5}g_{n-4}.
\]
Iterating gives \(\sum_{n=1}^{20}g_n=8659\). Including the empty set, the answer is \(8660\).

5. The prime exponents of \(10{,}080\) are \(5,2,1,1\). For exponent \(e\), the three exponents must have maximum \(e\) and minimum \(0\), giving \((e+1)^3-2e^3+(e-1)^3=6e\) assignments. Thus there are \(6^4(5\cdot2\cdot1\cdot1)=2592\) ordered triples. For any specified equal pair, there are \(2^4=16\) assignments satisfying the conditions; the three equal-pair cases are disjoint. Removing these 48 triples leaves \(2544\) ordered triples with distinct entries. Dividing by \(6\) gives \(424\).

6. The number of paths staying at or below the diagonal is
\[
\binom{20}{8}-\binom{20}{7}=48{,}450.
\]
A valid prefix to \((5,3)\) can be chosen in \(\binom83-\binom82=28\) ways. From \((5,3)\), the number of valid suffixes is \(\binom{12}{5}-\binom{12}{2}=726\), by reflecting paths that cross the boundary. Hence \(28\cdot726=20{,}328\) paths visit \((5,3)\), leaving \(28{,}122\).
