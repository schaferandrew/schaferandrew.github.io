I’ve used common Americanized spellings for the surnames, which can suit a U.S.-born character in a contemporary English-language novel. If you want the family background to feel more consciously Polish, you could restore diacritics: *Zieliński, Dąbrowski, Kamiński, Wójcik*, etc.

The strongest five for this particular character:

- **Katarzyna “Kasia” Wrona** — *Kasia* is a natural, lived-in Polish-American nickname rather than a costume. *Wrona* is short, sharp, and memorable; it has a slightly deadpan quality that suits dry humor.
- **Magdalena “Magda” Baran** — *Magda* feels intelligent, self-contained, and a little formal. *Baran* is sturdy and common enough to feel real, not exoticized. The name has a blunt rhythm.
- **Katherine “Kate” Kowalczyk** — *Kate* is period-plausible and Americanized, while *Kowalczyk* grounds the character in a Polish-American background without relying on the most obvious surname. It suits ambition and restraint.
- **Nora Sikora** — *Nora* is spare, clever, and emotionally guarded; *Sikora* is distinctive but not overly ornate. The full name feels quietly literary.
- **Helen Dabrowski** — *Helen* is strong, a bit old-fashioned, and class-plausible; *Dabrowski* is a solid, recognizable Polish surname. It sounds like a real Upper Midwest working-class name with backbone.
