The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while Mayor Theo Brandt called the measure "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." The levy still requires a second reading on March 24, after which collection would begin July 1 if it passes. Separately, columnist Rob Haddad argues in an opinion column that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing, "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. While an initial report stated two firefighters were injured, Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected the status, saying one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, though estimates of the crowd size varied. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300, police estimated the crowd at 400, and organizers of the rally said more than 1,000 people attended. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said, and the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
