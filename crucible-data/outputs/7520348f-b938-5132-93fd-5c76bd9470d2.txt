**Flagged content – Potentially unclear, overstated, or technically imprecise**

1. **“Inflation is when prices go up because the government prints too much money.”**  
   *Issue:* Simplifies inflation to a single cause; ignores demand‑side shocks, supply constraints, and expectations.

2. **“The CPI rises above 2% annually, the Federal Reserve is legally required to raise interest rates.”**  
   *Issue:* Misstates policy rule; Fed’s actions are discretionary, not legally mandated by CPI thresholds.

3. **“Anyone above that [natural unemployment] is either lazy or the victim of bad policy.”**  
   *Issue:* Value‑laden and unsupported claim; ignores structural unemployment, demographic shifts, etc.

4. **“Quantitative easing… inevitably causes hyperinflation.”**  
   *Issue:* Overstates QE’s effect; hyperinflation has not occurred in advanced economies despite QE.

5. **“Japan's experience with deflation proves that monetary policy is completely ineffective at stimulating demand.”**  
   *Issue:* Causal claim unsupported by evidence; other factors (structural, demographic) played roles.

6. **“Supply‑side economics has been definitively debunked… failed Reagan tax cuts…”**  
   *Issue:* Definitive language (“definitively debunked”) is too strong; the relationship between tax cuts, deficits, and growth is debated.

7. **General tone:** Several sentences contain informal or colloquial phrasing (e.g., “basically just printing money,” “lazy or victim of bad policy”) that may reduce academic neutrality.
