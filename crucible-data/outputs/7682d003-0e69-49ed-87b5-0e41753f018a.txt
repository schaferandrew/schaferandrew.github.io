The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called the move "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," though Council member Ines Varga voted no, stating the city had not published a cost estimate for households. If the levy passes its second reading on March 24, collection would begin July 1. Columnist Rob Haddad argues the levy is a "tax grab" that will cost a typical household $900 a year and claims the council rushed the vote without asking the people paying for it. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street. While an initial report from the scene indicated two firefighters were injured, Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected this, stating one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Estimates regarding the crowd size vary: Ledger reporters estimated about 300 people, police estimated 400, and rally organizers said more than 1,000 people attended. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, passing with votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. A spokesperson said Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign the bill this week, and the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%. This marks the third increase this year, cited as a response to inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
