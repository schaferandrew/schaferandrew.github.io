### The Top Five Choices

**1. Helena**
This is a strong choice because it bridges the gap between her heritage and her assimilation. "Helena" is deeply rooted in Polish culture (often paired with surnames like Kowalski or Mazur), yet it sounds fully Americanized and sophisticated enough for an ambitious character. It carries a regal, serious weight that fits her guarded nature without being overly exotic.

**2. Ruth**
Ruth is a classic Upper Midwest name, particularly common in working-class Catholic families of Polish descent. It conveys resilience and strength rather than softness. For a character who is emotionally guarded and ambitious, this name suggests someone who has endured hardship and survived it—a quiet, biblical fortitude that fits the literary tone.

**3. Clara**
Derived from the Latin for "bright" or "clear," this name subtly nods to her high intelligence without being ostentatious. It is simple enough to fit a working-class background but elegant enough for a literary protagonist. The sharpness of the 'C' and 'R' sounds aligns with her dry, witty humor.

**4. Eleanor**
Eleanor suggests ambition and seriousness. It is a name that implies history and weight, fitting for a character who has worked hard to elevate herself from a working-class background. It avoids being too trendy or soft, signaling a woman who is focused on the future rather than dwelling on the past.

**5. Beatrice**
This name offers a literary edge (referencing Dante's *Divine Comedy* or Beatrix Potter) that suits the genre of contemporary literary fiction. It implies wit and intelligence ("Bea" for short), which aligns with her dry humor. It is old-fashioned enough to feel grounded in her heritage but distinct enough to suggest she has carved out a unique identity.
