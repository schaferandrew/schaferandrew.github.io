a) **Emancipation Proclamation** – The proclamation took effect on Jan 1, 1863. By June 10 the farmer could have known about it.  
b) **Siege of Vicksburg** – The siege ran from May 18 to July 4, 1863. It was still in progress on June 10, so the farmer could have heard of it.  
c) **Lee’s defeat at Gettysburg** – The battle occurred July 1‑3, 1863. On June 10 Lee had not yet been defeated; the farmer could not have known this.  
d) **Telegraph news from Washington** – Telegraph lines were operational; Ohio farmers could receive such news.  
e) **Monitor vs. Merrimack** – The battle took place Mar 9, 1862 (“last year” relative to June 10, 1863). The farmer could have known about it.  
f) **Cousin telephoned with news** – Telephones were not invented until 1876. The farmer could not have received a telephone call in 1863.  
g) **Barbed‑wire fencing** – Barbed wire was invented in 1874. It did not exist for pasture fencing in 1863.  
h) **Mathew Brady’s Antietam photos** – The photographs were taken in Sept 1862 and were available by June 1863.  
i) **Stonewall Jackson’s death** – Jackson died May 10, 1863. The farmer could have known of his death “last month.”  
j) **West Virginia joining the Union** – West Virginia was admitted on June 20, 1863. On June 10 it had not yet joined; the farmer could not have known that it had joined “last month.”  
k) **Draft riots in New York** – The riots occurred July 13‑16, 1863. On June 10 they had not yet happened.
