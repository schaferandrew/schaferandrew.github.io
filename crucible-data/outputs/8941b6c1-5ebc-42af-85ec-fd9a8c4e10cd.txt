### 1. Twelfth roots of unity modulo \(N\)

Factor
\[
N=2^7\cdot3^4\cdot7^2\cdot13.
\]
Any solution must be a unit. By the Chinese remainder theorem, the counts for these four prime powers multiply.

For an odd prime power, the unit group is cyclic, so the number of twelfth roots is the gcd of \(12\) with the group order. These counts are
\[
\gcd(12,54)=6,\qquad \gcd(12,42)=6,\qquad \gcd(12,12)=12.
\]
Modulo \(2^7\), the unit group is \(C_2\times C_{32}\), giving
\[
\gcd(12,2)\gcd(12,32)=8
\]
roots. Thus the answer is
\[
8\cdot6\cdot6\cdot12=\boxed{3456}.
\]

### 2. Arrangements with no equal neighbors

Use inclusion–exclusion on the bonds between consecutive copies of each letter. For multiplicities \((m_1,m_2,m_3,m_4)=(4,3,2,2)\), choosing \(j_i\) bonds to merge leaves \(m_i-j_i\) blocks of letter \(i\). Hence the count is
\[
\sum_{j_1=0}^{3}\sum_{j_2=0}^{2}\sum_{j_3=0}^{1}\sum_{j_4=0}^{1}
(-1)^{j_1+j_2+j_3+j_4}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^4\binom{m_i-1}{j_i}\right)
\frac{(11-j_1-j_2-j_3-j_4)!}{\prod_{i=1}^4(m_i-j_i)!}.
\]
Collecting terms gives
\[
\frac{11!-22(10!)+190(9!)-840(8!)+2064(7!)
-2832(6!)+2016(5!)-576(4!)}{576}
=\boxed{6516}.
\]

### 3. Expected number of die rolls

Let \(t_i\) be the expected number of additional rolls when the previous roll was \(i\). A successful next roll contributes no further waiting time after that roll. The equations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
6t_1&=6+t_3+t_5,\\
6t_2&=6+t_2+t_4+t_6,\\
6t_3&=6+t_1+t_3+t_5+t_6,\\
6t_4&=6+t_2+t_4+t_5+t_6,\\
6t_5&=6+t_1+t_3+t_4+t_5,\\
6t_6&=6+t_2+t_3+t_4+t_6.
\end{aligned}
\]
Solving,
\[
(t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4,t_5,t_6)
=\frac1{61}(115,140,162,167,162,167).
\]
Including the first roll, the expectation is
\[
1+\frac16\sum_{i=1}^6t_i
=1+\frac{913}{366}
=\frac{1279}{366}.
\]
This fraction is reduced, so the answer is
\[
1279+366=\boxed{1645}.
\]

### 4. Subsets avoiding differences \(1\) and \(4\)

Encode a subset by a binary string of length \(20\). Track the last four bits, with the newest bit least significant. The possible masks are
\[
0,1,2,4,5,8,9,10.
\]
Appending \(0\) is always allowed. Appending \(1\) requires that both the newest and oldest tracked bits are \(0\), to avoid differences \(1\) and \(4\).

If the counts in the displayed mask order are
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_4,a_5,a_8,a_9,a_{10}),
\]
one transition gives
\[
(a_0+a_8,\ a_0,\ a_1+a_9,\ a_2+a_{10},\
a_2,\ a_4,\ a_4,\ a_5).
\]
Starting with \((1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)\), after \(20\) transitions the vector is
\[
(961,641,749,716,496,484,484,336).
\]
Its sum, including the empty subset, is \(\boxed{4867}\).

### 5. Triples with specified gcd and lcm

We have
\[
10080=2^5\,3^2\,5\,7.
\]
For a prime whose exponent in the lcm is \(e\), the three exponents must have minimum \(0\) and maximum \(e\). The number of ordered choices is
\[
(e+1)^3-2e^3+(e-1)^3=6e.
\]
Thus there are
\[
30\cdot12\cdot6\cdot6=12960
\]
ordered triples before requiring distinct entries.

For a specified equality, such as \(a=b\), each prime must have exponent pattern \((0,0,e)\) or \((e,e,0)\). There are \(2^4=16\) such triples. The three equality cases are disjoint, since all three entries cannot be equal. Removing them and dividing by \(6\) gives
\[
\frac{12960-3\cdot16}{6}=\boxed{2152}.
\]

### 6. Constrained lattice paths

By the reflection principle, the number of paths to \((12,8)\) never going above \(y=x\) is
\[
\binom{20}{8}-\binom{20}{7}=48450.
\]

To subtract paths through \((5,3)\), the number of valid prefixes is
\[
\binom83-\binom82=28.
\]
From \((5,3)\), a suffix uses seven right steps and five up steps. It starts two units below the diagonal, so reflection gives
\[
\binom{12}{5}-\binom{12}{2}=726
\]
valid suffixes. Therefore the required count is
\[
48450-28\cdot726=\boxed{28122}.
\]
