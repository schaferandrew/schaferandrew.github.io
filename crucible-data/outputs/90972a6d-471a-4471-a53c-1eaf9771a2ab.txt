Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years to fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. Columnist Rob Haddad argues that the levy is a tax grab, will cost a typical household $900 a year, and that the council rushed the vote. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore and was under control by 9:15 a.m. Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, correcting earlier reports that two firefighters were injured. The cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. The Ardmore Ledger estimated the crowd at about 300, police estimated it at 400, and organizers said more than 1,000 people attended. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. A spokesperson said Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, and the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, citing inflation of 4.1%. This is the third increase this year. [S8]
