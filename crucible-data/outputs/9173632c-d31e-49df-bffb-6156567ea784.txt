Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, with Mayor Theo Brandt calling it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation" and noting collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24, while Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, stated the city had not published a cost estimate for households; this proposal drew sharp criticism from The Ardmore Bugle's Rob Haddad, who argued in an opinion column that the levy is a tax grab costing a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote without asking the people paying for it. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, with crews gaining control by 9:15 a.m. and the cause remaining under investigation, though initial reports stating two firefighters were injured were later corrected by Fire Chief Ama Mensah, who confirmed at 11:40 a.m. that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with crowd estimates varying significantly between sources, as Ledger reporters at the scene estimated about 300 attendees while organizers claimed more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, with Governor Lena Ruiz expected to sign it this week so the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Separately, the central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]

Delphine Okoro won the open seat on the Port Ardmore school board with 52% of the vote, a result announced on March 3. [S10]
