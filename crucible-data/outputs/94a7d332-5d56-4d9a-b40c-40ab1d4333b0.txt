Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which would fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," but Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, criticized the lack of a published cost estimate for households. The levy still needs a second reading on March 24. Columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, saying "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. An initial report stated two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected this, saying one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., though the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with estimates of the crowd varying significantly. Ledger reporters estimated about 300 people, while organizers claimed more than 1,000 attended and police put the number at around 400. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, with the increase taking effect in the fall term. [S7]
