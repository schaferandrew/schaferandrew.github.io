

Reasoning checks:

1. Count multiples of 3 or 7 up to 10,000, then remove multiples of 21:
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{10000}{3}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{10000}{7}\right\rfloor-2\left\lfloor\frac{10000}{21}\right\rfloor
=3333+1428-2(476)=3809.
\]

2. Since \(7^4=2401\equiv 1\pmod{100}\), and \(2026\equiv 2\pmod 4\),
\[
7^{2026}\equiv 7^2=49\pmod{100}.
\]

3. Let \(a_n\) be the number of tilings of a \(2\times n\) board. The leftmost part is either a vertical domino, a \(2\times2\) square, or two horizontal dominoes, giving
\[
a_n=a_{n-1}+2a_{n-2},\quad a_0=1,\ a_1=1.
\]
Computing to \(n=12\):
\[
1,1,3,5,11,21,43,85,171,341,683,1365,2731.
\]
So \(a_{12}=2731\).

4. The largest die is exactly 4 iff all dice are at most 4 and at least one is 4:
\[
4^3-3^3=64-27=37.
\]
Total outcomes: \(6^3=216\). Probability:
\[
\frac{37}{216}.
\]

5. Each child has \(2\cdot 7=14\) equally likely sex/day combinations, so there are \(14^2=196\) ordered family outcomes.

Event \(E\): at least one child is a boy born on Tuesday.
\[
P(E)=1-\left(\frac{13}{14}\right)^2=\frac{27}{196}.
\]

Event \(B\): both children are boys. Among the \(7^2=49\) boy-boy day pairs, those with at least one Tuesday:
\[
7^2-6^2=49-36=13.
\]
Thus
\[
P(B\cap E)=\frac{13}{196}.
\]
Therefore
\[
P(B\mid E)=\frac{13/196}{27/196}=\frac{13}{27}.
\]
