## Where the brief conflicted with today's behavior — my calls

1. **No status concept existed; everything was public.** Default remains "publish immediately" so the RealWorld contract holds; only explicit `"status": "draft"` (anything else → 422, matching the client contract exactly) creates private work.
2. **404 vs 403 on unpublished articles.** The brief says "callers who aren't allowed get 403", but also "the public must not see… that it exists". A 403 would leak existence, so non-author/non-editor callers get **404** on drafts/in-review; 403 is reserved for callers who *can* see the article (e.g., editors approving their own work, or non-editors acting on a published one).
3. **Derived content**: tag listing, `?favorited=`, and the follow-feed now only count published articles, even for the favoriting/viewing user.
4. **Feedback lifecycle**: `submit` and `approve` clear `reviewComment` — the author's resubmission consumes the feedback (the brief doesn't specify; this keeps the "conversation" one item at a time).
5. **`GET /user/articles` shape**: the brief only specified `{articles, articlesCount}` for the queue; I used the same `ArticlesResponse` for consistency with every other listing endpoint.
6. **Review queue includes the editor's own in-review articles** (they still get 403 on approve/reject). Simpler and the article is their own; flag if the mobile team wants it excluded.

## What I'd flag in code review

- **Editors are usernames, resolved per request** — an editor who renames their account (PUT `/user`) silently loses the role; also case-sensitive match. Fine for in-memory config, worth a follow-up if this becomes persistent.
- **`db.editors` lives on the "database" object** because every layer only receives `Db`; acceptable for this architecture, but it's config, not data.
- **Body validation precedes 404** on reject (malformed body on an unknown slug → 422). Same pattern as existing routes.
- **Authors can still PUT/DELETE an in-review article** — edits are allowed mid-review; you may want to lock content once submitted.
- **Web nav shows "Review Queue" to every signed-in user**; non-editors see the 403 message instead of the queue (the web app has no editor field). Kept simple per the brief.
