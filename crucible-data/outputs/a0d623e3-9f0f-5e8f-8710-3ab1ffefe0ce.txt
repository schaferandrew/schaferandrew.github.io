### Why the top five stand out

| # | Name | Why it fits |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Mara Kline** | *Mara* is a strong, unpretentious name that feels Midwestern yet has a subtle Polish resonance (similar to “Marian”). *Kline* is a common American surname with no ethnic baggage, keeping the character grounded in her working‑class roots while sounding modern and professional. |
| 2 | **Elena Hartman** | *Elena* is a classic name that crosses cultural lines; it’s easy to pronounce, carries an intellectual vibe, and avoids overt Polish markers. *Hartman* suggests resilience and determination—qualities the character embodies—and fits comfortably in a Midwestern setting. |
| 3 | **Ruth Novak** | *Ruth* evokes steadiness and quiet strength, matching her guarded emotional nature. The surname *Novak* is of Slavic origin but common enough in America to feel ordinary; it hints at heritage without being stereotypical. |
| 4 | **Jade O’Connor** | *Jade* feels contemporary and sharp—perfect for a witty, ambitious protagonist. *O’Connor* is an Irish‑American surname that blends seamlessly into the Midwest’s diverse cultural tapestry, keeping her background subtle yet authentic. |
| 5 | **Lydia Shaw** | *Lydia* has literary weight (think Lydia Bennet) and carries an intellectual air. *Shaw* is a neutral, widely used surname that grounds the character in everyday America while allowing room for her dry humor to shine. |

These five names balance contemporary feel, Midwestern authenticity, and subtle nods to heritage without leaning into caricature—ideal for a nuanced literary protagonist.
