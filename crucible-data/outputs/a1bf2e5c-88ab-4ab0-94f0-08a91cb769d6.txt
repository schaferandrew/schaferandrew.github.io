**Reasoning.** For a working-class Polish-American from the upper Midwest (Minnesota/Wisconsin/Dakotas), authenticity comes less from exotic or heavily "ethnic" names and more from real surnames that actually appear in those communities—many descendants of mining, logging, and railroad towns—and first names that reflect how these families navigated assimilation. I've mixed plain-spoken American names (Mary, Anna) with ones that carry a touch of old-world texture (Beatrice, Sophie), varied the level of Polish-ness across the list so no single name signals heritage in an on-the-nose way, and leaned toward surnames with literary or sturdy resonance rather than caricature.

NAMES:

2. Anna Wozniak — timeless and plain-spoken, matching her guarded reserve; the surname is common enough to feel real rather than exotic.
3. Mary Nowak — Mary is the most common name ever, fitting a humble family perfectly while carrying weight for someone who's made her own way.
4. Barbara Szymanski — has that midwestern Polish-American cadence and slightly old-fashioned quality of a family that kept its traditions.
5. Helen Mazur — soft and unassuming like her guarded nature; the surname hints at heritage without being heavy-handed.

8. Agnes Jankowski — unglamorous and plain, fitting emotional guardedness; exactly what you'd hear in a small upper-Midwest town.
9. Rose Pawlak — nods to the immigrant generation but feels earned rather than decorative for someone who's moved beyond that identity.
10. Katherine Ziemkiewicz — bridges her intelligence and ambition with Polish roots; distinctive without being caricatured.
