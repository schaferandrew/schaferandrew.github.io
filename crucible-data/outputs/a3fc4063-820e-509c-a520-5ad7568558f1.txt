## Port Ardmore Briefing - Tuesday, March 10

**Most Important Stories:**

*   **Transit Levy Approved by City Council:** The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station (Ardmore Ledger). Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation" (Lakeshore Public Radio). The levy still requires a second reading on March 24. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, noted that the city had not published a cost estimate for households (Ardmore Ledger). Collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading (Lakeshore Public Radio).
*   **Kettle Street Warehouse Fire:** A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street. Initial reports stated two firefighters were injured (Statewide Wire). However, Fire Chief Ama Mensah later clarified that one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, correcting the earlier reports (Lakeshore Public Radio). Crews had control of the fire by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation (Lakeshore Public Radio).

**Other Local Items:**

*   **Rent Stabilization Rally:** Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Organizers claimed over 1,000 people attended, while police estimated the crowd at 400 (Lakeshore Public Radio).
*   **Teacher Pay Bill:** The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, passing by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. A spokesperson indicated Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, and the increase would take effect in the fall term (Statewide Wire).
