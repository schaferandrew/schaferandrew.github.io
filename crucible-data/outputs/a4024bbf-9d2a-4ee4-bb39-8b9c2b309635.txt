## Problem 1

Count multiples of 3 or 7 up to 10,000, then remove those divisible by 21 (which are the ones caught by both).

- Multiples of 3: ⌊10000/3⌋ = 3333
- Multiples of 7: ⌊10000/7⌋ = 1428
- Multiples of 21: ⌊10000/21⌋ = 476

Union (divisible by 3 **or** 7): $3333 + 1428 - 476 = 4285$.
Remove multiples of 21: $4285 - 476 = 3809$.

*(Check: div-by-3-not-7 = 2857; div-by-7-not-3 = 952; sum = 3809.)*

## Problem 2

Find the order of 7 mod 100:
$$7^2 = 49,\quad 7^3 \equiv 43,\quad 7^4 = 2401 \equiv 1 \pmod{100}.$$
So the cycle length is **4**. Since $2026 = 4\cdot 506 + 2$,
$$7^{2026} \equiv 7^2 = 49 \pmod{100}.$$

## Problem 3

Use a transfer-matrix / recurrence on columns. For a fresh column, the first cell forces one of:
- **Vertical domino** → leaves rest as $2\times(n-1)$ board; or
- **Two horizontals**, or **one square** (both consume columns 1–2) → leaves rest as $2\times(n-2)$ board.

This gives the recurrence $a_n = a_{n-1} + 2a_{n-2}$, with $a_0=1,\ a_1=1$. Closed form:
$$a_n = \frac{2^{n+1}+(-1)^n}{3}.$$

Check small cases: $a_1=1$ (vertical only), $a_2=3$ (two verticals, two horizontals, one square). ✓

For $n=12$:
$$a_{12} = \frac{2^{13}+(-1)^{12}}{3} = \frac{8192+1}{3} = 2729.$$

## Problem 4

Largest is exactly 4 ⟺ all dice ≤ 4 and at least one equals 4.
$$\#\{\text{all}\le 4\} - \#\{\text{all}\le 3\} = 4^3 - 3^3 = 64 - 27 = 37.$$
Total outcomes $= 6^3 = 216$, so the probability is $\dfrac{37}{216}$.

## Problem 5

Each child has 14 equally likely types (B/G × 7 days); two children give $14^2 = 196$ outcomes.

- **At least one boy-Tuesday:** total minus none — $(196 - 13^2) = 196 - 169 = 27$.
- **Both boys AND at least one Tuesday-boy:** $7^2 - 6^2 = 49 - 36 = 13$.

$$P(\text{both boys}\mid \text{at least one Tue-boy}) = \frac{13}{27}.$$
