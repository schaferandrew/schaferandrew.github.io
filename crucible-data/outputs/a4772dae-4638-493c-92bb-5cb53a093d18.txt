The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years to fund three new bus routes and rebuild the Fulton Street station, though council member Ines Varga raised concerns about the lack of published cost estimates for households. Mayor Theo Brandt praised this as "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," with collection set to begin July 1 if approved by its second reading on March 24.

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street, resulting in one firefighter being treated for smoke inhalation and released. The cause of the fire remains under investigation.

Supporters rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening for a proposed rent stabilization ordinance, with estimates ranging from 300 to over 1,000 attendees. Police estimated the crowd at around 400.

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, expected to be signed into law this week by Governor Lena Ruiz, taking effect in the fall term.

Veloria's central bank raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%.

Columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote without consulting those who would pay for it.

Delphine Okoro won the open seat on the Port Ardmore school board with 52% of the vote, though this result is from March 3rd and not included in today's briefing.
