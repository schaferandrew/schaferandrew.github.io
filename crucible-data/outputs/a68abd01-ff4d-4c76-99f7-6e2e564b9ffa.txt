The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy on a 7‑2 vote, a move that will fund three new bus routes and the rebuild of Fulton Street station; Mayor Theo Brandt hailed it as “the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation,” while council member Ines Varga opposed the measure because no household cost estimate had been published, and the levy still requires a second reading on March 24 before collection could begin July 1 if approved [S1, S2]. A fire erupted early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street; initial reports said two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later clarified that only one firefighter suffered smoke inhalation and was released, with the blaze under control by 9:15 a.m. and the cause still under investigation [S3, S4]. Supporters of a proposed rent‑stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening; while Ledger reporters estimated about 300 attendees, organizers claimed more than 1,000 people were present and police counted roughly 400 in the crowd [S5, S6]. The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39‑28 in the Senate and 81‑52 in the House; Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, with the increase slated for effect in the fall term [S7]. The central bank of Veloria lifted its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking its third hike this year amid a 4.1 % inflation reading [S8]. In an opinion column, Rob Haddad of *The Ardmore Bugle* criticized the transit levy as a “tax grab,” arguing it would cost a typical household $900 annually and that the council rushed the vote without adequate public input [S9].
