### The Top 5 Analysis

**1. Claire Marek**
This is the strongest "literary" choice. *Claire* is a classic, sturdy name that feels intelligent and understated. *Marek* is a common Polish surname that is easy for an English speaker to pronounce but retains its heritage. It suggests a woman who is polished but doesn't feel the need to explain herself.

**2. Beatrice "Bea" Nowak**
*Beatrice* carries a weight of intellect and tradition, fitting for a woman who is emotionally guarded. The nickname *Bea* provides that "dryly funny" accessibility. *Nowak* is one of the most common Polish surnames, grounding her in a very real, non-caricatured working-class history.

**3. Julia Szmidt**
*Julia* is a "bridge" name—it works perfectly in both a Polish and an American context. It feels ambitious and capable. *Szmidt* is sharp and percussive, matching a personality that might be a bit prickly or guarded.

**4. Theresa "Tess" Wojcik**
*Theresa* feels like a name a working-class Polish mother would choose, but *Tess* is the name the character has adopted for her professional life. It captures the transition from her roots to her current ambitions perfectly.

**5. Elena Vance**
This is a "soft" name for a "hard" character. *Elena* nods to her heritage without being overt, while *Vance* feels like a surname she might have taken or a family name that sounds more "upper-middle class" than her upbringing might suggest—highlighting her ambition to move beyond her origins.
