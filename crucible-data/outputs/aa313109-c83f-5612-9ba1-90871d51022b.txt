**Revised paragraph**

Mara stepped off the train and into a city that smelled like rain, hot asphalt, and somebody's breakfast. The station was louder **than** she remembered it, all brakes shrieking and people talking **too fast**. She could feel the old neighborhood somewhere beyond the buildings, waiting. Not waiting for her, exactly. That would have been too kind. Still—she was back. And she didn't know whether to laugh **or** leave.

**Changes made**

* **then → than** : comparative, not temporal. *louder than she remembered it*
* **talking to fast → talking too fast** : *too* is the adverb needed for degree, not the preposition *to*
* **whether to laugh, or leave → whether to laugh or leave** : the comma before *or* is unnecessary in a standard *whether...or* construction. Removed for clarity.

**Flagged as likely intentional — left as is**

* Sentence-initial **And** in “And she didn't know whether to laugh or leave.” — stylistic, kept.
* Fragment **“Not waiting for her, exactly.”** — intentional fragment for rhythm/voice, kept.
