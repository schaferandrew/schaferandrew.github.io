

The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy with a 7-2 vote, which will fund three new bus routes and the rebuilding of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," while Council member Ines Varga opposed the measure, stating the city had not published cost estimates for households. The levy requires a second reading on March 24 and, if approved, would begin collecting funds in July. Columnist Rob Haddad argued the levy is a tax grab, claiming it would cost households $900 annually and criticizing the rushed vote. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]  

A fire broke out at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street early Tuesday. Initial reports indicated two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later clarified that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, correcting earlier reports. The cause of the fire remains under investigation. [S3, S4]  

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance held a rally outside City Hall on Monday evening. The Ardmore Ledger estimated the crowd at about 300, while organizers claimed over 1,000 attendees and police reported an estimate of 400. [S5, S6]  

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, with votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign the bill this week, a spokesperson confirmed. [S7]  

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
