**a.** *Emancipation Proclamation* – took effect Jan 1 1863; a farmer reading newspapers in June 1863 would have heard of it.  

**b.** *Siege of Vicksburg* – was still underway (Mar 29 – Jul 4 1863); news could travel by rail or telegraph, so he could know about it.  

**c.** *Lee’s defeat at Gettysburg* – the battle occurred July 1‑3 1863, after June 10; the farmer could not have known of a victory that had not yet happened.  

**d.** *Telegraph news from Washington* – the telegraph was in regular use; newspapers could carry telegraph dispatches, so he might have heard about events in the capital.  

**e.** *Monitor vs. Merrimack* – the famous iron‑clad duel took place March 9 1862, a year earlier; information would already be circulating.  

**f.** *Cousin telephoned* – the telephone was not invented until 1876 (Bell’s patent 1876, commercial use early 1877); no telephone existed in 1863.  

**g.** *Barbed‑wire fence* – barbed wire was patented by Joseph Glidden in 1874; it could not have been used to fence a pasture in 1863.  

**h.** *Brady’s Antietam photographs* – the images were taken after the September 1862 battle and published in newspapers during 1863, so a farmer could have seen them.  

**i.** *Stonewall Jackson’s death* – he died May 10 1863 from wounds received at Chancellorsville; news would have reached Ohio within weeks.  

**j.** *West Virginia joined the Union last month* – West Virginia was admitted as a state on June 20 1863, ten days after June 10; it had not yet “joined” when the letter was written, so he could not have known that fact.  

**k.** *New York draft riots* – the major riots erupted July 13‑16 1863, well after June 10; they were not yet known.

**Anachronisms:** C,F,G,J,K
