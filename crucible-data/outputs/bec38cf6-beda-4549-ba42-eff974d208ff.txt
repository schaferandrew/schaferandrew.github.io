

1.  **3809**
    *   **Reasoning:** Let $A$ be the set of multiples of 3 and $B$ be the set of multiples of 7 in the range $[1, 10000]$. We want the number of integers in $A \cup B$ that are not in $A \cap B$ (multiples of 21).
    *   $|A| = \lfloor 10000/3 \rfloor = 3333$
    *   $|B| = \lfloor 10000/7 \rfloor = 1428$
    *   $|A \cap B| = \lfloor 10000/21 \rfloor = 476$
    *   The number of integers divisible by 3 or 7 is $|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B| = 3333 + 1428 - 476 = 4285$.
    *   Removing those divisible by 21 (which are exactly $A \cap B$): $4285 - 476 = 3809$.
    *   Alternatively, count multiples of 3 only ($3333 - 476 = 2857$) and multiples of 7 only ($1428 - 476 = 952$), then sum them: $2857 + 952 = 3809$.

2.  **49**
    *   **Reasoning:** We need $7^{2026} \pmod{100}$.
    *   Observe powers of 7 modulo 100:
        *   $7^1 \equiv 7$
        *   $7^2 \equiv 49$
        *   $7^3 \equiv 343 \equiv 43$
        *   $7^4 \equiv 7 \times 43 = 301 \equiv 1$
    *   The powers of 7 repeat with a period of 4.
    *   $2026 \div 4 = 506$ with a remainder of 2.
    *   Therefore, $7^{2026} \equiv 7^2 \equiv 49 \pmod{100}$.

3.  **2731**
    *   **Reasoning:** Let $a_n$ be the number of ways to tile a $2 \times n$ board.
    *   To tile the rightmost part of the board:
        *   Place a vertical domino ($2 \times 1$): leaves a $2 \times (n-1)$ board ($a_{n-1}$ ways).
        *   Place two horizontal dominoes ($1 \times 2$ stacked): leaves a $2 \times (n-2)$ board ($a_{n-2}$ ways).
        *   Place one square ($2 \times 2$): leaves a $2 \times (n-2)$ board ($a_{n-2}$ ways).
    *   Recurrence: $a_n = a_{n-1} + 2a_{n-2}$.
    *   Base cases: $a_0 = 1$ (empty board), $a_1 = 1$ (one vertical domino).
    *   Sequence: 1, 1, 3, 5, 11, 21, 43, 85, 171, 341, 683, 1365, 2731.
    *   Alternatively, using the closed form $a_n = \frac{2^{n+1} + (-1)^n}{3}$, for $n=12$: $\frac{2^{13} + 1}{3} = \frac{8193}{3} = 2731$.

4.  **37/216**
    *   **Reasoning:** The total number of outcomes when rolling 3 dice is $6^3 = 216$.
    *   The event "largest number is exactly 4" means all dice show numbers $\le 4$, and at least one die shows a 4.
    *   Number of outcomes where all dice are $\le 4$ is $4^3 = 64$.
    *   Number of outcomes where all dice are $\le 3$ is $3^3 = 27$.
    *   The number of outcomes where the maximum is exactly 4 is $64 - 27 = 37$.
    *   Probability is $37/216$. (37 is prime, so the fraction is in lowest terms).

5.  **13/27**
    *   **Reasoning:** This is a variation of the Boy or Girl paradox.
    *   Total possible states for two children (Gender, Day of Birth) is $(2 \times 7) \times (2 \times 7) = 196$.
    *   Condition: At least one child is a Boy born on a Tuesday (BT).
    *   Number of outcomes satisfying the condition: Total outcomes - Outcomes with no BT.
    *   Outcomes with no BT: Each child can be any of the 13 states that are not BT (Girl any day: 7, Boy not Tuesday: 6). So $13 \times 13 = 169$.
    *   Outcomes with at least one BT: $196 - 169 = 27$.
    *   Target event: Both children are boys (BB).
    *   We need the number of outcomes where both are boys AND at least one is BT.
    *   Total outcomes where both are boys: $7 \times 7 = 49$ (days can be anything).
    *   Outcomes where both are boys but NO BT: Both are Boys born on non-Tuesdays. $6 \times 6 = 36$.
    *   Outcomes where both are boys and at least one is BT: $49 - 36 = 13$.
    *   Probability is $13/27$.
