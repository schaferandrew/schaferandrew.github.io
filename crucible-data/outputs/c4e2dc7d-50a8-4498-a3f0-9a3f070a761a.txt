I looked for first names that were plausible in the late 1980s and early 1990s in Catholic-influenced, working-class households—leaning toward biblical and saints’ names, but also crisp, serious names that can sound like emotional armor. For surnames, I chose authentic Polish names that are common in the upper Midwest (Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee, Cleveland, the Twin Cities) but avoided the most cartoonishly overwrought or joke-prone options; the goal is heritage that feels lived-in and ordinary rather than performed for effect.
