I’ve favored first names plausible for someone born in the early 1990s and Polish-American surnames that suggest heritage without making it the character’s defining feature. The reasons below concern how each name reads on the page, rather than what it necessarily says about a person.

NAMES:
1. Megan Kaczmarek — An everyday first name grounds the distinctive surname; believable across both her hometown and professional life.
2. Claire Nowak — Spare and composed, with an understated sharpness that suits the novel’s tone.
3. Erin Mazur — Unshowy and brisk; feels natural for someone whose humor arrives without warning.
4. Natalie Witek — A fluid, approachable name that offers useful contrast with her emotional reserve.
5. Allison Urban — Familiar and socially adaptable; works equally well on a childhood class roster and an office door.
6. Julia Lis — Quietly elegant without sounding grand; its brevity leaves room for a complicated character.
7. Rebecca Wilk — Conventional and substantial, with believable nickname options that could distinguish family from colleagues.
8. Sarah Baran — Deliberately ordinary, allowing her intelligence and ambition to emerge through action rather than naming.
9. Laura Dudek — Understated and credible for her generation; avoids making either class or heritage feel overemphasized.
10. Rachel Krupa — A conversational rhythm with a crisp finish, well suited to dry dialogue.
11. Emily Sikora — A familiar generational first name paired with a surname that carries heritage unobtrusively.
12. Anna Wrona — Simple and restrained, with a slight formality that works for an emotionally guarded protagonist.
13. Katherine Sowa — Offers a useful divide between professional “Katherine” and a family’s persistent “Katie.”
14. Nicole Pawlak — Grounded in her age cohort, with an unvarnished familiarity that suits the setting.
15. Andrea Sobczak — Adult and matter-of-fact; feels convincing in both family arguments and professional negotiations.
16. Dana Ostrowski — Compact and unsentimental, with a strong rhythm that never feels theatrical.
17. Jillian Kurek — Slightly polished but still familiar; “Jill” gives her a less formal register at home.
18. Monica Zajac — Distinctive without seeming selected to announce her personality or ancestry.
19. Lauren Michalski — Smooth and conventional, useful for a character whose outward competence conceals considerable tension.
20. Alexandra Borkowski — The contrast between “Alexandra” and “Alex” gives her different registers without requiring an elaborate backstory.

TOP FIVE:
1. Erin Mazur
2. Megan Kaczmarek
3. Claire Nowak
4. Allison Urban
5. Natalie Witek
