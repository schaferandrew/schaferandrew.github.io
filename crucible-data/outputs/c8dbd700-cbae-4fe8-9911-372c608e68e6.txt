The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, funding three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, and the levy still needs a second reading on March 24. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading. In an opinion column, columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing, "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. An initial report from the scene said two firefighters were injured, but Fire Chief Ama Mensah later said earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect; one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., and the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said. The increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300; organizers said more than 1,000 people attended, while police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
