**a) The Emancipation Proclamation taking effect this New Year’s Day** – It had already taken effect on January 1, 1863; by June 10 it was known.  

**b) The siege of Vicksburg dragging on** – The siege was still ongoing in early July 1863, so the farmer could have heard about its continuation.  

**c) News of Lee’s defeat at Gettysburg** – Gettysburg ended on July 3, 1863; no news of a Union victory existed on June 10, 1863.  

**d) The telegraph bringing news from Washington** – Telegraph service was well‑established by 1863 and could deliver news quickly.  

**e) The Monitor’s fight with the Merrimack last year** – That battle occurred in March 1862; its outcome would be known months earlier.  

**f) A cousin in Cleveland who telephoned with the news** – The telephone was not invented until 1876, so a phone call could not have happened in 1863.  

**g) Fencing the north pasture with barbed wire** – Barbed wire was patented only in 1874; it did not exist in 1863.  

**h) Mathew Brady’s photographs of the dead at Antietam** – The battle took place in September 1862 and its photos were already circulating by mid‑1863.  

**i) Stonewall Jackson's death last month** – He died on May 31, 1864; that event was still months away from June 10, 1863, so it could not have been known then.  

**j) West Virginia, which joined the Union last month** – West Virginia had already become a state in May 1863, so its admission would be known by early June.  

**k) The draft riots in New York** – Those occurred in January 1863; their news would have been circulating well before June 10.
