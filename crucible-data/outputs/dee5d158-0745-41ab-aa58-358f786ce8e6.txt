The Port Ardmore City Council approved a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years late Monday on a 7-2 vote, with Mayor Theo Brandt calling it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." The measure, which needs a second reading on March 24, will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station, though collection would begin July 1 if it passes. Council member Ines Varga voted against the levy, stating the city had not published a cost estimate for households, while columnist Rob Haddad argued in an opinion piece that the levy is a "tax grab" that will cost a typical household $900 a year and was rushed through. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire that broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore resulted in one firefighter being treated for smoke inhalation and released, according to Fire Chief Ama Mensah, who clarified that earlier reports stating two firefighters were injured were incorrect. Crews brought the blaze under control by 9:15 a.m., though the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with estimates of the crowd size varying significantly, as the Ardmore Ledger reported an estimated crowd of about 300, while organizers claimed more than 1,000 people attended and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, with Governor Lena Ruiz expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said, and the increase taking effect in the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
