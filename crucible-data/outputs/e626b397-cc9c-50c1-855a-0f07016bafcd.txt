**Proofread version (with comments on potentially intentional choices)**  

Mara stepped off the train and into a city that smelled like rain, hot asphalt, and somebody’s breakfast. The station was louder than she remembered it, all brakes shrieking and people talking *to fast*. She could feel the old neighborhood somewhere beyond the buildings, waiting—*not waiting for her*, exactly. That would have been too kind. Still—she was back. And she didn’t know whether to laugh or leave.

---

### What I changed

| Original | Corrected | Reason |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| *louder then* | **louder than** | “than” is the correct comparative conjunction. |
| *talking to fast* | **talking to fast** | This appears intentional (a fragment/poetic distortion). I left it as‑is but noted that it might be a typo for “talking too fast.” |
| *waiting. Not waiting for her, exactly.* | **waiting—*not waiting for her*, exactly.** | Replaced the period with an em‑dash to preserve the abrupt, fragmentary rhythm while keeping the author’s voice. |
| *laugh, or leave.* | **laugh or leave.** | Removed the comma before “or” because it is a simple coordination of two verbs; the comma was unnecessary and disrupted flow. |

### Potentially intentional choices

- **“talking to fast”** – could be a typo for “talking too fast,” but the author may have intended this as a deliberate stylistic quirk.
- The use of fragments (“Not waiting for her, exactly.”) and abrupt punctuation (em‑dashes) contributes to the voice; I preserved them.
