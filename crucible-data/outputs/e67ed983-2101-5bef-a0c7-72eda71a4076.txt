a) **Emancipation Proclamation taking effect this New Year’s Day** – The proclamation went into force on January 1, 1863, so the farmer could certainly know about it.

b) **The siege of Vicksburg dragging on** – The siege began May 18, 1863 and continued past June 10; news of its progress would have been circulating.

c) **News of Lee’s defeat at Gettysburg** – Gettysburg was fought July 1‑3, 1863. On June 10 the battle had not yet occurred, so this could not be known.

d) **The telegraph bringing news from Washington** – Telegraph lines were operational and routinely carried news from Washington to Ohio in 1863.

e) **The Monitor’s fight with the Merrimack last year** – The Battle of Hampton Roads (Monitor vs. Merrimac) took place March 9, 1862; it was common knowledge by mid‑1863.

f) **A cousin in Cleveland who telephoned with the news** – The telephone was not invented until 1876, so a phone call could not have happened.

g) **Fencing the north pasture with barbed wire** – Barbed wire was patented in 1874; it did not exist in 1863.

h) **Mathew Brady’s photographs of the dead at Antietam** – The Battle of Antietam occurred September 17, 1862 and Brady’s images were widely seen by mid‑1863.

i) **Stonewall Jackson’s death last month** – Jackson died May 10, 1863; a reference to “last month” would be accurate on June 10.

j) **West Virginia, which joined the Union last month** – West Virginia was admitted to the Union on June 20, 1863. On June 10 it had not yet joined.

k) **The draft riots in New York** – The New York Draft Riots took place July 13‑18, 1863; they could not be reported on June 10.
