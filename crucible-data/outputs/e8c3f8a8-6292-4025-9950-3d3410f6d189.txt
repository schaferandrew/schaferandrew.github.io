a) **Possible.** The Emancipation Proclamation took effect on **January 1, 1863**, so “this New Year’s Day” fits.

b) **Possible.** The siege of Vicksburg began in **May 1863** and continued until July 4. It was underway on June 10.

c) **Anachronism.** The Battle of Gettysburg occurred on **July 1–3, 1863**. Lee’s defeat was still in the future.

d) **Possible.** Telegraph networks were well established by 1863 and could carry news from Washington to Ohio.

e) **Possible.** The *Monitor* fought the Confederate *Virginia*, commonly called the *Merrimack*, on **March 9, 1862**—the previous year.

f) **Anachronism.** Practical telephone service did not exist in 1863; Bell’s telephone patent came in **1876**.

g) **Anachronism.** Commercially practical barbed-wire fencing emerged later, becoming widely available in the **1870s**. Fencing an Ohio pasture with it in June 1863 would be out of place.

h) **Possible.** Photographs of Antietam’s dead were exhibited at **Mathew Brady’s New York gallery in October 1862**. They were associated with Brady, although Alexander Gardner and James Gibson took the battlefield photographs.

i) **Possible.** Stonewall Jackson died on **May 10, 1863**, exactly one month before the letter’s date.

j) **Anachronism.** West Virginia entered the Union on **June 20, 1863**—ten days after the letter, not “last month.”

k) **Anachronism.** The New York City draft riots occurred on **July 13–16, 1863**, more than a month after the letter.

ANACHRONISMS: c, f, g, j, k
