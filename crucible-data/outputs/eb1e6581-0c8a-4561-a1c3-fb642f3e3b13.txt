

1. Multiples of 3: \(\lfloor10000/3\rfloor=3333\).  
   Multiples of 7: \(\lfloor10000/7\rfloor=1428\).  
   Multiples of 21: \(\lfloor10000/21\rfloor=476\).  
   Divisible by 3 or 7 but not 21:
   \[
   3333+1428-2\cdot476=3809.
   \]

2. Since \(7^4\equiv 1\pmod{100}\), and \(2026\equiv 2\pmod4\),
   \[
   7^{2026}\equiv 7^2\equiv 49\pmod{100}.
   \]

3. For a \(2\times n\) board, let \(a_n\) be the number of tilings.  
   The leftmost column can be filled either by one vertical domino (\(a_{n-1}\) ways) or by a width-2 block, which can be either two horizontal dominoes or one \(2\times2\) square (\(2a_{n-2}\) ways). Thus
   \[
   a_n=a_{n-1}+2a_{n-2},\quad a_0=1,\ a_1=1.
   \]
   Computing to \(n=12\):
   \[
   a_{12}=2731.
   \]

4. The largest die is exactly 4 iff all dice are at most 4 and at least one is 4:
   \[
   4^3-3^3=64-27=37.
   \]
   Total outcomes: \(6^3=216\).  
   Probability:
   \[
   \frac{37}{216}.
   \]

5. Each child has \(2\cdot7=14\) equally likely possibilities: sex and birthday.  
   Condition “at least one is a boy born on Tuesday” leaves
   \[
   14^2-13^2=196-169=27
   \]
   ordered cases.  
   Both children are boys with at least one Tuesday boy:
   \[
   7^2-6^2=49-36=13.
   \]
   Probability:
   \[
   \frac{13}{27}.
   \]

ANSWERS
1. 3809
2. 49
3. 2731
4. 37/216
5. 13/27
