The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, according to the Ardmore Ledger. The money would fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. Lakeshore Public Radio quoted Mayor Theo Brandt calling the levy "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." The levy still needs a second reading on March 24, and collection would begin July 1 if it passes. In an opinion column, Rob Haddad of The Ardmore Bugle calls the levy a "tax grab." He argues it will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote. That $900 figure is his own claim and does not appear in the news reports. [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

The state legislature has passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, according to the Statewide Wire. The Senate voted 39-28 and the House 81-52. A spokesperson said Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, and the increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, and the sources disagree on the crowd size. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated about 300 people. Police estimated 400. Organizers said more than 1,000 attended. [S5, S6]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street. The Statewide Wire's initial report said two firefighters were injured. Fire Chief Ama Mensah later said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, and that the earlier reports of two injuries were incorrect. Lakeshore Public Radio reported that crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. The cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]
