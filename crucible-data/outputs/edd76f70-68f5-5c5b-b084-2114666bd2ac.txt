### Claims, Terminology, and Technical Imprecision

**1. "Inflation is when prices go up because the government prints too much money."**
*   **Technical Imprecision:** This is a reductionist definition. While monetary expansion is a primary driver of inflation, inflation can also be caused by supply-side shocks (e.g., oil price spikes), increased aggregate demand (non-monetary), or changes in the velocity of money.
*   **Overstated:** Attributing inflation solely to "printing money" ignores the complexities of monetary theory.

**2. "...if there's more money chasing the same amount of stuff, each dollar is worth less."**
*   **Unclear/Informal:** The phrase "stuff" is imprecise for an economics essay. A more technical term would be "goods and services" or "aggregate supply."

**3. "...when CPI rises above 2% annually, the Federal Reserve is legally required to raise interest rates."**
*   **Factually Incorrect:** The Federal Reserve is not "legally required" to raise interest rates based on a specific CPI threshold. The Fed operates under a "dual mandate" (maximum employment and stable prices) and uses discretionary policy; there is no statutory mandate requiring a rate hike at exactly 2%.

**4. "...the natural rate of unemployment is around 4%, which means anyone above that is either lazy or the victim of bad policy."**
*   **Overstated/Subjective:** The claim that individuals above the natural rate are "lazy" is a subjective value judgment, not an economic fact. 
*   **Technical Imprecision:** The "natural rate of unemployment" (NAIRU) is a theoretical concept that varies by country, time period, and economic conditions; it is not a fixed universal constant of 4%.

**5. "Quantitative easing... is basically just printing money to buy treasury bonds..."**
*   **Technical Imprecision:** While often described as "printing money," QE is more accurately described as an expansion of the central bank's balance sheet through the purchase of assets. It involves the creation of electronic reserves rather than physical currency.

**6. "...which devalues the currency and inevitably causes hyperinflation."**
*   **Overstated/Factually Incorrect:** QE does not "inevitably" cause hyperinflation. Hyperinflation is a specific, extreme phenomenon (usually 50%+ per month) that requires a total collapse of monetary credibility, which has not occurred in the US following QE.

**7. "Japan's experience with deflation in the 1990s proves that monetary policy is completely ineffective at stimulating demand."**
*   **Overstated/Unclear:** The word "completely" is an absolute that is rarely supported in economics. While Japan’s "liquidity trap" is a significant case study, economists debate the extent to which monetary policy was ineffective versus the role of structural issues and the "zero lower bound."

**8. "...supply-side economics has been definitively debunked by the failed Reagan tax cuts, which increased the deficit without growing the economy at all."**
*   **Overstated/Factually Incorrect:** The term "definitively debunked" is an extreme rhetorical stance. Economists remain divided on the long-term effects of Reaganomics. Furthermore, the claim that they did not grow the economy "at all" contradicts standard GDP data from that era, though the *extent* of that growth relative to the deficit increase is a subject of ongoing debate.
