The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy, which will fund three new bus routes and the rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Mayor Theo Brandt called the levy "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation," and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading on March 24. However, Council member Ines Varga voted no, stating that the city had not published a cost estimate for households. A columnist in *The Ardmore Bugle* argued that the levy represents a tax grab, estimating it would cost a typical household $900 a year and claiming the council rushed the vote without asking the people paying for it. [S1, S2, S9]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street, resulting in initial reports of two injured firefighters. However, Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected this, stating that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released. Crews had brought the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., though the cause remains under investigation. [S3, S4]

Regarding local issues, supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall Monday evening; while Ledger reporters estimated the crowd at about 300, organizers claimed that more than 1,000 people attended, and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

On a broader scale, the state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 after votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, with Governor Lena Ruiz expected to sign it this week for implementation in the fall term. [S7]

Separately, the central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, marking the third increase this year in response to 4.1% inflation. [S8]

Finally, the Port Ardmore school board election saw Delphine Okoro win the open seat with 52% of the vote. [S10]
