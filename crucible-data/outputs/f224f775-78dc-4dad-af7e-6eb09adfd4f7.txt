**A note on approach:** She'd have been named by immigrant or first-generation parents around 1990, so I'm favoring real names that could plausibly appear in an upper-Midwest working-class home—avoiding both blandly generic English names and "ethnic" names that read as costume. Each pairs a Polish-origin first name (often the saint-names families chose) with a surname that either keeps her heritage visible or signals blended/working-class roots, so every option carries a bit of the tension between her guarded ambition and where she came from.

NAMES:
1. Anna Kowalski — Timeless and common; the kind of name that survives generations without pretension or caricature, fitting someone who blends in while quietly standing out.
2. Hanna Miller — Hanna (Polish Anne) keeps a subtle heritage marker while Miller anchors her firmly in working-class America.
3. Barbara O'Connell — A saint-name chosen for respectability by first-generation immigrants; the mixed surname hints at blended roots.
4. Teresa Gallagher — Typical of families naming daughters after saints; sturdy, unpretentious, and grounded.
5. Catherine Wojciechowski — Catherine reads as educated and integrated, while the distinctive Polish surname keeps her identity intact.
6. Marie Novak — Marie carries a French-flavored elegance many Polish families adopted; refined yet working-class.
7. Ewa Vojcik — Ewa is distinctly Polish and literary; Vojcik (anglicized Wojciech) is clean and unpretentious.
8. Zofia Kozlowska — Zofia (Sophie) is unmistakably Polish and sophisticated, with a double-syllable surname suggesting intact heritage.
9. Alice Mazur — Alice reads as old-money literary, but paired with the distinctly Polish Mazur it creates tension between guarded ambition and roots.
10. Wanda Kowalczyk — Working-class and unapologetically Polish; suits someone who won't pretend at gentility.
11. Dorota Nowak — Dorota (Dorothy) is a common immigrant name that feels both ordinary and quietly strong.
12. Ruth O'Brien — Ruth was beloved in Catholic/Polish families for its biblical weight; the surname suggests mixed heritage.
13. Lidia Szymanski — Lidia is distinctive and literary, slightly unusual—fits an intelligent woman who stands out without trying.
14. Magdalena Lewandowski — Magdalena (Maggie/Magda) is common but authentic; the full form feels deliberate and dignified.
15. Natalie Halikowski — Natalie is a bridge name both Polish families loved and that reads as American and European at once.
16. Joanna Dabrowski — Joanna (Joan/Julia) is distinctly Polish, yet the working-class surname keeps her grounded despite the elegant name.
17. Klara Zaremba — Klara (Clara) has an old-world, dryly witty quality; the distinctive surname grounds her in heritage without caricature.
18. Helen Krawczyk — Helen is the anglicized Halina; familiar but paired with a cobbler's surname it feels real rather than generic.
19. Bessie Kamiński — Bessie signals working-class roots plainly, for someone who won't hide her background.
20. Gabriela Jankowski — Gabriela (Gabi) is softer and more literary; intelligent and slightly unusual without being exotic.
