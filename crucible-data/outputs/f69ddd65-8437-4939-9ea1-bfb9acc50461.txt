**a) Emancipation Proclamation taking effect this New Year's Day: Possible.** It took effect January 1, 1863, so "this New Year's Day" fits a letter from June 1863.

**b) The siege of Vicksburg dragging on: Possible.** Grant began the siege on May 18, 1863, and Vicksburg did not surrender until July 4. On June 10 it was very much dragging on.

**c) News of Lee's defeat at Gettysburg: Anachronism.** The battle was fought July 1–3, 1863, three weeks after the letter's date.

**d) The telegraph bringing news from Washington: Possible.** The telegraph had been in wide use since the 1840s and was the main channel for war news.

**e) The Monitor's fight with the Merrimack last year: Possible.** The Battle of Hampton Roads took place March 8–9, 1862, which was "last year."

**f) A cousin in Cleveland who telephoned: Anachronism.** Alexander Graham Bell patented the telephone in 1876, thirteen years later.

**g) Fencing the north pasture with barbed wire: Anachronism.** Practical barbed wire came in the late 1860s and 1870s, with Joseph Glidden's patent in 1874. An 1863 farmer would have used rail, board, stone, or plain wire fencing.

**h) Mathew Brady's photographs of the dead at Antietam: Possible.** Brady exhibited "The Dead of Antietam" in his New York gallery in October 1862, and it was widely reported. The photos were actually taken by his employees Alexander Gardner and James Gibson.

**i) Stonewall Jackson's death last month: Possible.** Jackson died May 10, 1863, after being wounded at Chancellorsville.

**j) West Virginia, which joined the Union last month: Anachronism.** West Virginia became a state on June 20, 1863, ten days after the letter. Statehood had been authorized, but it had not "joined last month."

**k) The draft riots in New York: Anachronism.** They occurred July 13–16, 1863, a month after the letter's date.

ANACHRONISMS: c, f, g, j, k
