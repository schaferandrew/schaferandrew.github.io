**Port Ardmore briefing — Tuesday, March 10**

**Transit levy approved by City Council**
* Ardmore Ledger, March 10, 7:05 a.m.: The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years. The money will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station. Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households. The levy still needs a second reading on March 24.
* Lakeshore Public Radio, March 10, 8:20 a.m.: Port Ardmore's council backed the transit levy on a 7-2 vote. Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." Collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading.

**Kettle Street warehouse fire**
* Statewide Wire, March 10, 6:10 a.m.: A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore. An initial report from the scene said two firefighters were injured. The cause is under investigation.
* Lakeshore Public Radio, March 10, 11:40 a.m., update: Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released; earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect. Crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m. The cause remains under investigation.

**Rent stabilization rally, Monday evening**
Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening.
* Ardmore Ledger, March 10, 9:30 a.m.: Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300.
* Lakeshore Public Radio, March 10, 9:45 a.m.: Organizers of Monday's rent stabilization rally said more than 1,000 people attended. Police estimated the crowd at 400.
Crowd size is reported differently by the three sources.

**State teacher pay bill**
* Statewide Wire, March 10, 10:00 a.m.: The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House. Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said. The increase would take effect in the fall term.

**Opinion, separate from reporting**
* The Ardmore Bugle, opinion, March 10: Columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote. "Nobody asked the people paying for it," he writes.
