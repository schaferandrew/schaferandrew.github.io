The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years to fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station, and Mayor Theo Brandt called it "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation." Council member Ines Varga, who voted no, said the city had not published a cost estimate for households, the levy still needs a second reading on March 24, and collection would begin July 1 if the levy passes its second reading. Columnist Rob Haddad argues that the transit levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, writing "Nobody asked the people paying for it." [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

A fire broke out early Tuesday at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore and crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., with the cause remaining under investigation. Fire Chief Ama Mensah said one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, and that earlier reports that two firefighters were injured were incorrect. [S3, S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening. Ledger reporters at the scene estimated the crowd at about 300, organizers of Monday's rent stabilization rally said more than 1,000 people attended, and police estimated the crowd at 400. [S5, S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500, by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, and Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week, a spokesperson said. The increase would take effect in the fall term. [S7]

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1%. [S8]
