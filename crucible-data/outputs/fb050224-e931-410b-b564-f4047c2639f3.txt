The Port Ardmore City Council voted 7-2 late Monday to approve a $41.6 million transit levy spread over ten years, which will fund three new bus routes and a rebuild of the Fulton Street station, a measure championed by Mayor Theo Brandt as "the biggest investment in buses this city has made in a generation" and set to begin collection on July 1 pending a second reading on March 24, while council member Ines Varga warned that the city had not published a cost estimate for households; columnist Rob Haddad argues the levy will cost a typical household $900 a year and that the council rushed the vote, noting "Nobody asked the people paying for it" [S1, S2, S9 opinion]

An early Tuesday fire broke out at a vacant warehouse on Kettle Street in Port Ardmore, and initial reports said two firefighters were injured according to an initial report from the scene [S3]; Fire Chief Ama Mensah later corrected that only one firefighter was treated for smoke inhalation and released, saying earlier reports of two injuries were incorrect, and crews had the fire under control by 9:15 a.m., with the cause remaining under investigation [S4]

Supporters of a proposed rent stabilization ordinance rallied outside City Hall on Monday evening, with Ledger reporters estimating the crowd at about 300 [S5]; organizers claimed more than 1,000 people attended [S6], while police estimated the crowd at 400 [S6]

The state legislature passed a bill raising the minimum teacher salary to $48,500 by votes of 39-28 in the Senate and 81-52 in the House, and Governor Lena Ruiz is expected to sign it this week according to a spokesperson [S7], with the increase taking effect in the fall term

The central bank of Veloria raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.75%, its third increase this year, citing inflation of 4.1% [S8]
