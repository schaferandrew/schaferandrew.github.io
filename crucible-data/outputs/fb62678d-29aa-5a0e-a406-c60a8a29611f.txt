### Strongest five for highly intelligent, dryly funny, ambitious and emotionally guarded

**1. Anna Nowak**
Anna is the classic Polish name that has been fully naturalized in the US for over a century. It’s simple, unadorned and timeless — no frills, no softening diminutives. It reads as intelligent and self-contained, and the crisp two syllables suit a dry wit and emotional guardedness.

**2. Catherine "Cate" Wozniak**
Catherine is a traditional Polish-American name, but the nickname Cate gives it a sharp, clipped edge. The hard C and T sounds feel precise and a little wry, which fits dry humor and ambition without warmth on the surface.

**3. Margaret "Margo" Kowalski**
Margaret is a mid-century staple in Polish-American working-class families. Margo as the used name feels classic but unsentimental — mature, competent, and quietly ambitious. It suggests someone who has chosen a cooler public persona.

**4. Helena Dabrowski**
Helena is authentically Polish but also long-established in American usage, often shortened to Helen. It carries quiet elegance without being ornate, and the full form feels reserved and guarded while still intelligent.

**5. Elizabeth "Liz" Piotrowski**
Elizabeth is the ultimate adaptable classic, and Liz is the no-nonsense version you’d hear in Milwaukee or Green Bay. It signals intelligence and professionalism, with the flexibility to be warm in private and guarded in public — perfect for a dry humorist who doesn’t overshare.

All five keep the heritage present in the surname while using first names that would be completely ordinary for a woman her age in the Upper Midwest, avoiding exoticization or caricature.
